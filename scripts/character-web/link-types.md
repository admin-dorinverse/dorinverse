# Character web: link types

The standard every link in `links.json` is written and reviewed against. Agreed with the owner on 2026-10-31 and revised 2026-11-01.

**Scripture has to prove every connection.** This is the Bible: the cited passages are the proof of why two people are connected, so that no one can question it. A connection states the main reason, or reasons, the two are connected, not small details that don't matter to their story overall.

A link **summarizes a relationship.** It is not a record of everything that happened between two people. Look at their whole story and ask: what was their relationship, ultimately? The label states that.

## How every link is decided

1. **Relationship first.** Read the whole story between the two people and their key moments, across the whole span of it: how long and how often they dealt with each other. Several moments of the same kind over years (Nathan advising David, bringing him God's word, coming to him about the throne) show a lasting relationship, not separate incidents. Write in 1–3 plain sentences what the relationship ultimately was. Only then choose a label.
2. **Usually one label.** Use two only when two things truly defined the relationship, e.g. David served Saul, and Saul then hunted him. Use more only in rare cases like Judas and Jesus (Apostle, Followed, Betrayed). Never give a label to a single passing incident. When two people met only once, and that meeting itself had a clear nature, it gets that label (Elisha healed Naaman: Healed). A moment that is not the relationship can be mentioned in the sentence, or left out.
3. **Cite the passages that show the relationship.** As many as it takes, usually one to three.
4. **The sentence sums up the relationship** in plain words. It says only what scripture says, and makes clear who did what. The label states the fact, and the sentence gives the direction ("Nathan rebuked David over Bathsheba").
5. **Scripture only.** No tradition, no guessing, nothing invented. Never claim scripture makes something the heart of a relationship unless it actually says so; that is opinion. If scripture itself names the relationship ("enemies", "his servant", "loved"), use it.
6. **Meeting.** Two people don't have to have met if scripture shows a real relationship between them: Jezebel had Naboth killed by her letters (Enemies), a descendant (Family), a prophecy fulfilled in someone. But sharing a story, ruling one after the other, or one simply hearing of the other is not a relationship, and gets no link.
7. **Never force a label.** If no label below honestly states the relationship, do not group the pair under the nearest one. Flag it as needing a new label, with the relationship and its passages; a new label is added to this sheet before it is used. Crossed paths is not a fallback.

## The labels

| Label | key | Across their story… | Not this |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Family** | `family` | they were related by blood, adoption or marriage (in-laws), and scripture states the tie outright (not pieced together from two verses). Always given when they're family, even when something else also defines the relationship (Jacob and Laban: Family + Served) | Husband and wife (→ Married) |
| **Married** | `married` | they were husband and wife. That is enough: spouses don't also get Loved, or Family even if they were also related (Abraham and Sarah) | — |
| **Loved** | `loved` | their bond was love or deep devotion, as scripture says: Jesus loved Lazarus, Jonathan loved David, Ruth clave to Naomi | Spouses (→ Married). A kind act (→ Helped) |
| **Helped** | `helped` | one stood by, protected, rescued or provided for the other, and that marks their relationship | A small favour in passing. Help given as someone's servant (→ Served) |
| **Worked together** | `worked_together` | they were partners in the same work: Paul and Barnabas, Ezra and Nehemiah | Simply being on the same side, or in the same place |
| **Counseled** | `counseled` | one was the other's adviser or guide | One piece of advice in passing |
| **Apostle** | `apostle` | Jesus and one of the twelve, always together with Followed | Anyone else |
| **Followed** | `followed` | one was the other's disciple: followed them and learned from them | — |
| **Served** | `served` | one served under the other: a king and his official, a master and his servant, a commander and his soldier | Believers as servants of God in general. A one-time hire |
| **Anointed** | `anointed` | one anointed or set the other apart for God's work, and that is their bond | A king giving someone a post (→ Served) |
| **Blessed** | `blessed` | one blessed the other in the LORD's name, and that marks their relationship: Eli blessed Hannah twice, and both prayers were answered | A greeting in passing. Setting apart for God's work (→ Anointed) |
| **Rebuked** | `rebuked` | confronting the other over sin is what their relationship comes down to | One sharp word in a story that is about something else |
| **Enemies** | `enemies` | they were enemies: one sought to harm, defeat or destroy the other, or scripture calls them enemies | Competing for something (→ Rivals) |
| **Betrayed** | `betrayed` | one turned against someone who trusted them | A trick on a stranger. Ordinary hostility (→ Enemies) |
| **Rivals** | `rivals` | they competed for the same thing: love, a blessing, standing, a throne | Seeking to destroy each other (→ Enemies) |
| **Healed** | `healed` | one healed, raised or restored the other by God's power, and that is their connection | — |
| **Friends** | `friends` | scripture calls them friends or companions (Job's three friends; Daniel's companions; Pilate and Herod "made friends") | Being on the same side (→ nothing). Partners in a work (→ Worked together) |
| **Covenant** | `covenant` | they made a covenant or sworn agreement with each other, and that marks their relationship (Abimelech with Abraham at Beersheba) | A family or marriage tie |
| **Judged** | `judged` | one sat in judgment on the other: tried them, or passed sentence on them (Pilate and Jesus; Felix and Paul; Solomon sentencing Joab) | Carrying out a death sentence (→ Put to death) |
| **Put to death** | `put_to_death` | one put the other to death at a king's or ruler's command (Benaiah and Joab) | Killing in battle or out of enmity (→ Enemies) |
| **Saved** | `saved` | scripture says salvation came to one through the other ("This day is salvation come to this house"; "words, whereby thou… shalt be saved") | Healing (→ Healed) |
| **Deceived** | `deceived` | one lied to the other to take from them, and that is their connection (Gehazi and Naaman) | Turning on someone who trusted them (→ Betrayed) |
| **Prophesied** | `prophesied` | one spoke the LORD's word to or about the other, and no passage says it was fulfilled in them (Amos against Jeroboam; Simeon over the child Jesus) | Scripture connecting the words to them (→ Prophecy fulfilled) |
| **Prophecy fulfilled** | `prophecy` | one of them spoke a prophecy, and scripture connects it to the other: it says the words were fulfilled in them, or that it happened "according to the word of the LORD, which he spake by" the prophet, or names them as the one the prophet spoke of ("this is he that was spoken of by the prophet Esaias") | The story merely matching the words, with no passage connecting them |
| **Crossed paths** | `crossed_paths` | they just crossed paths: they met, and that meeting was all there was between them, with no other nature to it | A relationship no label fits (→ flag it for a new label, rule 7) |
