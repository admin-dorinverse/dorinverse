How to read an entry:
- **Title** — after the book name, what the player learns from the whole flavor, as this recipe teaches and tests it (2.10).
- **Range** — the verses the flavor teaches and reveals (Question Rules 2.12 format).
- **Parallel** — other accounts of the same events: revealed after the base, never a question source (2.17).
- **Frame** — verses outside the range that lesson 1 also draws on (Letters: who wrote it, to whom, and why, 7.L2).
- **Lessons** — one numbered line per lesson: its range, then its title in plain words (2.10). The line under it says what the lesson teaches toward the flavor title.
- **Split option** — where the flavor could become two flavors that each still have 2–5 lessons (2.4), or "None".
- **People / Places** — who and where the reference layer links to the range, most-mentioned first.
- **Turn** — the words in the text where the section begins.

## Romans

### 1. Romans: The Good News Paul Preached
- **Range:** Romans 1:1–17
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Romans 1:1–7 — Paul Writes to Rome
     - Paul says who he is, who he writes to, and that he was set apart for God’s good news about His Son (1:1–4).
  2. Romans 1:8–17 — Not Ashamed of the Gospel
     - What the good news is: God’s power to save everyone who believes (1:16–17). Paul’s wish to visit Rome is folded in with no questions (2.14).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (4):** God, Jesus Christ, David, Paul
- **Places (1):** Rome
- **Turn:** Opening greeting: "Paul, a servant of Jesus Christ… To all that be in Rome" (1:1, 1:7), then "I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ" (1:16).

### 2. Romans: All Stand Guilty Before God
- **Range:** Romans 1:18–3:20
- **Frame:** Romans 1:1–7
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Romans 1:18–32 — People Who Turned Away from God
     - The first group Paul shows guilty: people who knew God but did not honour Him (1:21).
  2. Romans 2:1–16 — God Judges Everyone Fairly
     - Those who judge others are guilty too, because God judges each person by what they have done (2:1, 2:6).
  3. Romans 2:17–29 — Having God’s Law Is Not Enough
     - The Jews, who had the law, are guilty too when they break it (2:23).
  4. Romans 3:1–20 — No One Is Righteous
     - The argument’s end: “all the world” is guilty before God, and the law cannot make anyone right (3:19–20).
- **Split option:** At 2:17 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (2):** God, Jesus Christ
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** "For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of men" (1:18).

### 3. Romans: Made Right with God by Faith
- **Range:** Romans 3:21–5:21
- **Frame:** Romans 1:1–7
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Romans 3:21–31 — A Gift for All Who Believe
     - The answer to the last flavor: God makes people right through faith in Jesus Christ, for all who believe (3:22).
  2. Romans 4:1–12 — Abraham Believed God
     - The proof case: Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness (4:3).
  3. Romans 4:13–25 — Father of Many Nations
     - The promise to Abraham came through faith, not the law, and is counted to all who believe (4:13, 4:24).
  4. Romans 5:1–11 — Peace with God
     - What those made right by faith now have: peace with God and hope (5:1–2).
  5. Romans 5:12–21 — One Man’s Sin, One Man’s Gift
     - How one man’s obedience makes many righteous, as one man’s sin brought death (5:12, 5:19).
- **Split option:** At 4:13 (2 + 3 lessons); or at 5:1 (3 + 2 lessons).
- **People (8):** God, Jesus Christ, Abraham, Adam, David, Holy Spirit, Moses, Sarah
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** "But now the righteousness of God without the law is manifested" (3:21).

### 4. Romans: Grace Is No Excuse to Sin
- **Range:** Romans 6
- **Frame:** Romans 1:1–7
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Romans 6:1–14 — Dead to Sin, Alive to God
     - Answers “Shall we continue in sin?” (6:1): those joined to Christ have died to sin and now live to God (6:11).
  2. Romans 6:15–23 — Whose Servant You Are
     - Answers the question again (6:15): you serve whatever you obey; sin pays death, but God gives eternal life (6:16, 6:23).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (2):** God, Jesus Christ
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** "What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid" (6:1–2).

### 5. Romans: No Condemnation in Christ
- **Range:** Romans 7–8
- **Frame:** Romans 1:1–7
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Romans 7:1–13 — The Law Is Holy
     - The law is holy and shows sin for what it is, but it cannot give life (7:7, 7:12).
  2. Romans 7:14–25 — The Struggle with Sin
     - A person who wants to do good under the law but cannot, ending “who shall deliver me?” and “I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord” (7:19, 7:24–25).
  3. Romans 8:1–11 — Life by God’s Spirit
     - The answer: “There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus”; God did what the law could not, and His Spirit gives life (8:1–3, 8:11).
  4. Romans 8:12–30 — Suffering Now, Glory Later
     - Those led by the Spirit are God’s children and heirs, who “suffer with him, that we may be also glorified together”; present sufferings are not worth comparing with the glory to come, and all things work together for good to those who love God (8:14–18, 8:28).
  5. Romans 8:31–39 — Nothing Can Separate Us
     - The hope ends in a promise: nothing can separate believers from God’s love in Christ (8:38–39).
- **Split option:** Romans 7:1–8:11 (lessons 1–3) and Romans 8:12–39 (lessons 4–5), cut at “Therefore, brethren, we are debtors” (8:12).
- **People (3):** God, Holy Spirit, Jesus Christ
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** "Know ye not, brethren… how that the law hath dominion over a man as long as he liveth?" (7:1).
- **Notes:** Re-cut: Romans 7:1–8:17 and Romans 8:18–39 used to be two flavors, but 8:18 goes on from 8:17 with “For I reckon,” so they are joined. Romans 7 asks the question (7:24) that Romans 8 answers. Lessons in Romans 8 are cut at “Therefore, brethren” (8:12) and “What shall we then say to these things?” (8:31), so no lesson ends between 8:17 and 8:18.

### 6. Romans: God Keeps His Word

- **Frame:** Romans 1:1–7
- **Lessons:** 5

  2. Romans 9:14–33 — God’s Mercy Is His to Give
     - God shows mercy as He chooses, and Israel stumbled by seeking righteousness by works, not faith (9:15, 9:32).
  3. Romans 10 — Salvation for All Who Call

  4. Romans 11:1–15 — A Remnant Chosen by Grace
     - God has not cast away His people; there is a remnant chosen by grace (11:1–2, 11:5).
  5. Romans 11:16–36 — The Olive Tree and Its Branches
     - Gentiles grafted into the olive tree, “all Israel shall be saved”, mercy on all, and praise for God’s wisdom (11:17, 11:26, 11:32–33).
- **Split option:** At 10:1 (2 + 3 lessons); or at 11:1 (3 + 2 lessons).
- **People (16):** God, Jesus Christ, Isaiah, Jacob (Israel), Moses, Abraham, Isaac, Baal, Benjamin (patriarch), David, Elijah, Esau (Edom), Holy Spirit, Hosea, Pharaoh (of the Exodus), Sarah
- **Places (3):** Zion, Gomorrah, Sodom
- **Turn:** Change of subject to Israel: "I have great heaviness and continual sorrow in my heart… for my brethren, my kinsmen according to the flesh" (9:2–3); title from 10:1.

### 7. Romans: A Life Given to God

  4. Romans 13:8–14 — Love Does What the Law Asks

- **Places (0):** —

### 8. Romans: When Believers Disagree

- **Places (8):** Jerusalem, Spain, Achaia, Asia, Cenchreae, Illyricum, Judea, Macedonia
- **Turn:** New subject: "Him that is weak in the faith receive ye, but not to doubtful disputations" (14:1).
- **Notes:** Folded in: Romans 15:14–16:27 (plans and greetings) was its own flavor; 2.14 keeps no lesson on greetings or plans. 16:22 names Tertius, “who wrote this epistle”; 1:1 names Paul as the sender, so lessons name Paul as the writer and mention Tertius only in the words of 16:22.

## 1 Corinthians

### 9. 1 Corinthians: The Wisdom of the Cross
- **Range:** 1 Corinthians 1–2
- **Frame:** 1 Corinthians 1:10–11
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. 1 Corinthians 1:1–9 — Enriched by God’s Grace
     - Who wrote and to whom, and the grace God gave them in Christ, “in all utterance, and in all knowledge” (1:4–5).
  2. 1 Corinthians 1:10–17 — A Church Split over Leaders
     - The problem the cross answers: divisions over Paul, Apollos and Cephas, and Paul refusing to preach “with wisdom of words” (1:12, 1:17).
  3. 1 Corinthians 1:18–31 — Why the Cross Seems Foolish
     - The cross is foolishness to the world but God’s power and wisdom, so no one may glory except in the Lord (1:18, 1:31).
  4. 1 Corinthians 2:1–16 — Wisdom Taught by God’s Spirit
     - Paul preached Christ crucified, and God’s wisdom is known only by His Spirit (2:2, 2:10).
- **Split option:** At 1:18 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (11):** God, Jesus Christ, Holy Spirit, Paul, Apollos, Chloe, Crispus, Gaius, Simon Peter, Sosthenes, Stephanas
- **Places (1):** Corinth
- **Turn:** Opening greeting: "Paul, called to be an apostle… and Sosthenes our brother, Unto the church of God which is at Corinth" (1:1–2).
- **Notes:** Co-sender Sosthenes is named (1:1). The "why" (contentions "declared unto me… by them which are of the house of Chloe", 1:11) sits in lesson 2, so 1:10–11 is the frame for lesson 1 and joins the greeting in the frame of every later 1 Corinthians flavor.

## 2 Corinthians
