How to read an entry:
- **Title** — after the book name, what the player learns from the whole flavor, as this recipe teaches and tests it (2.10).
- **Range** — the verses the flavor teaches and reveals (Question Rules 2.12 format).
- **Parallel** — other accounts of the same events: revealed after the base, never a question source (2.17).
- **Frame** — verses outside the range that lesson 1 also draws on (Letters: who wrote it, to whom, and why, 7.L2).
- **Lessons** — one numbered line per lesson: its range, then its title in plain words (2.10). The line under it says what the lesson teaches toward the flavor title.
- **Split option** — where the flavor could become two flavors that each still have 2–5 lessons (2.4), or "None".
- **People / Places** — who and where the reference layer links to the range, most-mentioned first.
- **Turn** — the words in the text that tie the flavor’s poems together, or where a long poem’s section begins.

## Psalms

### 1. Psalms: The Life God Blesses
- **Range:** Psalm 1; Psalm 15; Psalm 24; Psalm 26; Psalm 28; Psalm 34
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 1; Psalm 15 — A Tree Planted by the Rivers
     - The blessed man delights in the LORD’s law and is “like a tree planted by the rivers of water”, while the ungodly are chaff; the one who may dwell on God’s holy hill “speaketh the truth in his heart” (1:2–4; 15:1–2).
  2. Psalm 24 — Clean Hands and a Pure Heart
     - The earth is the LORD’s; the one who may stand in His holy place has “clean hands, and a pure heart”, and the gates lift up their heads for “the King of glory” (24:1, 24:3–4, 24:7).
  3. Psalm 26; Psalm 28 — Not Swept Away with the Wicked
     - The speaker has walked in integrity and loved God’s house, and asks not to be gathered with sinners; the next poem prays, “Draw me not away with the wicked”, and ends in thanks (26:1, 26:8–9; 28:3, 28:6–7).
  4. Psalm 34 — Taste and See That God Is Good
     - A poor man cried and the LORD saved him; whoever wants long life keeps his tongue from evil and will “seek peace, and pursue it”, for “The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart” (34:6, 34:8, 34:12–14, 34:18).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 26 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (2):** God, Jacob (Israel)
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** Each poem shows the person God blesses: “Blessed is the man” (1:1); “He shall receive the blessing from the LORD” (24:5).

### 2. Psalms: The LORD’s Anointed King
- **Range:** Psalm 2; Psalms 20–21; Psalm 45; Psalm 72; Psalm 101; Psalm 110
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Psalm 2 — Kings Who Rage Against God
     - The kings of the earth plot “against the LORD, and against his anointed”, but He laughs, sets His king on Zion, and warns them to “Serve the LORD with fear” (2:2, 2:4, 2:6, 2:11).
  2. Psalms 20–21 — Some Trust in Chariots
     - The people pray for their king in the day of trouble, saying “we will remember the name of the LORD our God”; then the king rejoices in God’s strength, because “the king trusteth in the LORD” (20:1, 20:7, 21:1, 21:7).
  3. Psalm 45 — A Song Written for the King
     - A song “touching the king”: he is “fairer than the children of men” and rides out for truth and meekness, and the king’s daughter is brought to him “in raiment of needlework” (45:1–2, 45:4, 45:13–14).
  4. Psalm 72 — A King Who Defends the Poor
     - A prayer that the king and the king’s son rule with God’s righteousness: “He shall judge the poor of the people”, save the needy, and all nations shall serve him (72:1, 72:4, 72:11–13).
  5. Psalm 101; Psalm 110 — Rule in the Midst of Enemies
     - One vows to walk “with a perfect heart” and cut off wicked doers from the city of the LORD; then the LORD says to another, “Sit thou at my right hand”, and swears, “Thou art a priest for ever” (101:2, 101:8; 110:1–2, 110:4).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 45 (2 + 3 lessons).
- **People (6):** God, Jesus Christ, David, Jacob (Israel), Jesse, Melchisedec
- **Places (7):** Zion, Sheba, Lebanon, Ophir, Seba, Tarshish, Tyre
- **Turn:** God names the king His own: “Yet have I set my king upon my holy hill of Zion” (2:6).

### 3. Psalms: Morning and Night with God
- **Range:** Psalms 3–5; Psalm 59; Psalm 92; Psalm 143
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Psalms 3–4 — Sleeping in Peace Among Enemies
     - Many say there is no help for him in God, yet the speaker lies down and sleeps, “for the LORD sustained me”; in the second poem, “I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep” (3:2, 3:5; 4:8).
  2. Psalm 5 — Morning Prayer for Guidance
     - The speaker says, “in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee”, and asks the LORD to lead him, because God takes no pleasure in wickedness and will bless the righteous (5:3–4, 5:8, 5:12).
  3. Psalm 59 — Enemies Who Prowl at Evening
     - Enemies come back each evening, making “a noise like a dog” around the city, but the speaker will “sing aloud of thy mercy in the morning” (59:6, 59:14, 59:16).
  4. Psalm 92 — Flourishing Like a Palm Tree
     - It is good to give thanks, showing God’s “lovingkindness in the morning, and thy faithfulness every night”; the wicked spring up like grass, but “The righteous shall flourish like the palm tree” (92:1–2, 92:7, 92:12).
  5. Psalm 143 — Led into the Land of Uprightness
     - Crushed by the enemy, the speaker stretches out his hands like “a thirsty land” and asks, “Cause me to hear thy lovingkindness in the morning”, and “Teach me to do thy will” (143:3, 143:6, 143:8, 143:10).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 59 (2 + 3 lessons).
- **People (2):** God, Jacob (Israel)
- **Places (1):** Lebanon
- **Turn:** The poems pray at the turn of the day: “I laid me down and slept; I awaked; for the LORD sustained me” (3:5).

### 4. Psalms: When Death Comes Close
- **Range:** Psalm 6; Psalm 30; Psalm 41; Psalm 88; Psalms 116–117
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Psalm 6 — Tears All Night Long
     - Weak and grieving, the speaker floods his bed with tears and asks, “in the grave who shall give thee thanks?”, then knows “the LORD hath heard the voice of my weeping” (6:2, 6:5–6, 6:8).
  2. Psalm 30 — Mourning Turned into Dancing
     - The LORD brought his soul up from the grave; His anger lasts a moment, and “weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning” (30:3, 30:5, 30:11).
  3. Psalm 41 — Kept Alive Through Sickness
     - The LORD will strengthen the one who considers the poor “upon the bed of languishing”; enemies say he will not rise, and even “mine own familiar friend” has turned against him (41:1, 41:3, 41:8–9).
  4. Psalm 88 — Crying Out from the Dark
     - The speaker has cried “day and night”, his life draws near the grave, and he asks, “shall the dead arise and praise thee?”; the poem ends still in darkness (88:1, 88:3, 88:10, 88:18).
  5. Psalms 116–117 — Paying Vows for Life Saved
     - “The sorrows of death compassed me”, but the LORD delivered his soul from death, so he asks, “What shall I render unto the LORD”; then all nations are called to praise (116:3, 116:8, 116:12; 117:1).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 41 (2 + 3 lessons).
- **People (1):** God
- **Places (1):** Jerusalem
- **Turn:** The poems face the grave: “For in death there is no remembrance of thee” (6:5).

### 5. Psalms: Hated Without a Cause
- **Range:** Psalm 7; Psalm 35; Psalm 69; Psalm 109
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Psalm 7 — The Pit the Wicked Dug
     - Chased by one who would tear him like a lion, the speaker asks God to judge him by his integrity; the wicked man “made a pit, and digged it, and is fallen into the ditch which he made” (7:2, 7:8, 7:15).
  2. Psalm 35 — Repaid Evil for Good
     - False witnesses rose up and “rewarded me evil for good”, though he mourned for them when they were sick; he asks the LORD to plead his cause (35:1, 35:11–14).
  3. Psalm 69:1–21 — Sinking in Deep Waters
     - The waters have come in to his soul; those who “hate me without a cause” outnumber the hairs of his head, and in his thirst “they gave me vinegar to drink” (69:1–2, 69:4, 69:21).
  4. Psalm 69:22–36 — The LORD Hears the Poor
     - He asks God to repay his enemies, “Let their table become a snare”, then turns to praise, for “the LORD heareth the poor” and will save Zion (69:22, 69:30, 69:33, 69:35).
  5. Psalm 109 — Cursed by Men, Blessed by God
     - Liars repay his love with hatred; he asks that the cursing they loved come back on them, and trusts that the LORD “shall stand at the right hand of the poor” (109:2–5, 109:17, 109:28, 109:31).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 69 (2 + 3 lessons).
- **People (4):** God, Jacob (Israel), Judah (patriarch), Satan
- **Places (1):** Zion
- **Turn:** The same words recur: “hate me without a cause” (69:4); “fought against me without a cause” (109:3).

### 6. Psalms: Creation Shows God’s Glory
- **Range:** Psalm 8; Psalm 19; Psalm 33; Psalm 104
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 8; Psalm 19 — Skies That Speak of God
     - Looking at the moon and stars, the speaker asks, “What is man, that thou art mindful of him?”; “The heavens declare the glory of God”, and “The law of the LORD is perfect” (8:3–4; 19:1, 19:7).
  2. Psalm 33 — He Spoke and It Was Done
     - “By the word of the LORD were the heavens made”; “he spake, and it was done”, and no king is saved by an army, but the LORD’s eye is on those who fear Him (33:6, 33:9, 33:16, 33:18).
  3. Psalm 104:1–18 — Waters Given Their Bounds
     - God covers Himself with light and stretches out the heavens; He set the waters a bound they may not pass, sends springs into the valleys, and makes grass grow for the cattle (104:2, 104:9–10, 104:14).
  4. Psalm 104:19–35 — All Creatures Wait on God
     - The moon marks the seasons, young lions seek their meat from God, the sea teems with creatures, and “These wait all upon thee”; when He sends His spirit, “they are created” (104:19, 104:21, 104:25, 104:27, 104:30).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 104 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (1):** God
- **Places (1):** Lebanon
- **Turn:** The poems open on creation: “O LORD our Lord, how excellent is thy name in all the earth!” (8:1).

### 7. Psalms: God Is the Judge
- **Range:** Psalm 9; Psalm 50; Psalm 58; Psalm 75; Psalms 82–83
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 9 — A Refuge for the Oppressed
     - The LORD sits on His throne judging right; He “will be a refuge for the oppressed”, and the nations sink into the pit they made (9:4, 9:9, 9:15).
  2. Psalm 50 — Offer God Thanksgiving
     - God calls heaven and earth to judge His people; He needs no bulls, for “the cattle upon a thousand hills” are His, so “Offer unto God thanksgiving” (50:4, 50:10, 50:14, 50:23).
  3. Psalm 58; Psalm 75 — Unjust Judges Brought Down
     - Rulers who do not judge uprightly are like a deaf adder, yet “verily he is a God that judgeth in the earth”; “God is the judge: he putteth down one, and setteth up another” (58:1, 58:4, 58:11; 75:7).
  4. Psalms 82–83 — Rise Up and Judge the Earth
     - God tells unjust rulers, “Defend the poor and fatherless”; the next poem asks Him not to keep silence while nations plot against Israel, “That men may know” He is most high over all the earth (82:3, 82:8; 83:1, 83:4, 83:18).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 58 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (13):** God, Asaph (Levite), Amalek, Ben-ammi, Jabin (king of Hazor), Jacob (Israel), Lot, Moab, Oreb, Sisera (army captain), Zalmunna, Zebah, Zeeb
- **Places (8):** Zion, Asshur, Edom, En-dor, Gebal, Kishon, Midian, Tyre
- **Turn:** The poems name God as judge: “for God is judge himself” (50:6); “But God is the judge” (75:7).

### 8. Psalms: Nothing Is Hidden from God
- **Range:** Psalm 10; Psalm 14; Psalm 53; Psalm 94; Psalm 139
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 10 — The Wicked Say God Forgets
     - The proud wicked hunt the poor and say in their hearts, “God hath forgotten: he hideth his face”, but “Thou hast seen it”, and “thou art the helper of the fatherless” (10:2, 10:11, 10:14).
  2. Psalm 14; Psalm 53 — The Fool Who Says No God
     - Two nearly matching poems: “The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God”; God looks down from heaven and finds “none that doeth good, no, not one” (14:1–3; 53:1–3).
  3. Psalm 94 — He Who Made the Eye Sees
     - The wicked say, “The LORD shall not see”, but “he that formed the eye, shall he not see?”; when the speaker’s foot slipped, “thy mercy, O LORD, held me up” (94:7, 94:9, 94:18).
  4. Psalm 139 — Known and Searched by God
     - The LORD knows his sitting down and rising up and every word on his tongue; there is nowhere to flee from His presence, for “the darkness hideth not from thee”, and he ends, “Search me, O God” (139:1–4, 139:7, 139:12, 139:23).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 94 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (2):** God, Jacob (Israel)
- **Places (1):** Zion
- **Turn:** The wicked speak in their hearts: “He hath said in his heart, God hath forgotten” (10:11); “The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God” (14:1).

### 9. Psalms: Putting Trust in the LORD
- **Range:** Psalm 11; Psalm 13; Psalm 16; Psalm 23; Psalms 54–56; Psalms 141–142
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Psalm 11; Psalm 13 — Holding On When God Seems Far
     - Told to “Flee as a bird to your mountain”, the speaker answers that “The LORD is in his holy temple”; in the next poem he asks, “How long wilt thou forget me”, yet ends, “I have trusted in thy mercy” (11:1, 11:4; 13:1, 13:5).
  2. Psalm 16; Psalm 23 — The Shepherd Who Provides
     - “The LORD is the portion of mine inheritance and of my cup”, who shows “the path of life”; and “The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want”, even in “the valley of the shadow of death” (16:5, 16:11; 23:1, 23:4).
  3. Psalms 54–55 — Cast Your Burden on the LORD
     - Strangers rise against the speaker, but “God is mine helper”; in the second poem a close friend has turned on him, and he says, “Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee” (54:3–4; 55:12–14, 55:22).
  4. Psalm 56 — Fear Answered with Trust
     - Enemies twist his words every day, but “What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee”; he asks God, “put thou my tears into thy bottle” (56:3, 56:5, 56:8, 56:11).
  5. Psalms 141–142 — When No One Cares for the Soul
     - He prays, “Set a watch, O LORD, before my mouth”, and asks to be kept from evil men; then, when “no man cared for my soul”, he cries, “Thou art my refuge” (141:3–4; 142:4–5).
- **Split option:** At Psalms 54–55 (2 + 3 lessons).
- **People (2):** God, Jesus Christ
- **Places (1):** Gath
- **Turn:** The poems choose trust: “In the LORD put I my trust” (11:1); “in thee do I put my trust” (16:1).

### 10. Psalms: Tongues Sharp as Swords
- **Range:** Psalm 12; Psalm 52; Psalm 64; Psalm 140
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Psalm 12; Psalm 52 — Flattering Lips Cut Off
     - People speak “with flattering lips and with a double heart”, but “The words of the LORD are pure words”; a mighty man boasts in mischief with a tongue “like a sharp razor”, while the speaker is “like a green olive tree in the house of God” (12:2, 12:6; 52:1–2, 52:8).
  2. Psalm 64 — Bitter Words Like Arrows
     - The wicked “whet their tongue like a sword” and shoot bitter words in secret, but God shoots them with an arrow, and “they shall make their own tongue to fall upon themselves” (64:3–4, 64:7–8).
  3. Psalm 140 — The LORD Defends the Afflicted
     - Violent men sharpen their tongues like a serpent, and “adders’ poison is under their lips”, but the speaker knows the LORD “will maintain the cause of the afflicted” (140:1, 140:3, 140:12).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (1):** God
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** The poems open on lying speech: “They speak vanity every one with his neighbour” (12:2).

### 11. Psalms: Shelter Under God’s Wings
- **Range:** Psalm 17; Psalm 36; Psalm 57; Psalm 61; Psalm 91
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 17 — The Apple of God’s Eye
     - Asking God to hear a prayer from honest lips, the speaker says, “Keep me as the apple of the eye, hide me under the shadow of thy wings”, safe from enemies like a greedy lion (17:1, 17:8, 17:12).
  2. Psalm 36 — The Fountain of Life
     - The wicked man has “no fear of God before his eyes”, but God’s mercy reaches the heavens, people trust “under the shadow of thy wings”, and “with thee is the fountain of life” (36:1, 36:5, 36:7, 36:9).
  3. Psalm 57; Psalm 61 — A Heart Fixed on God
     - Among lions, the speaker takes refuge “in the shadow of thy wings” until trouble passes, and his heart is fixed to sing; in the next poem he asks, “lead me to the rock that is higher than I” (57:1, 57:4, 57:7; 61:2, 61:4).
  4. Psalm 91 — Safe in the Secret Place
     - Whoever dwells “in the secret place of the most High” is covered with His feathers; a thousand may fall at his side, and God promises, “I will be with him in trouble” (91:1, 91:4, 91:7, 91:15).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 57 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (1):** God
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** The poems share one picture: “hide me under the shadow of thy wings” (17:8).

### 12. Psalms: Rock, Fortress, and Deliverer
- **Range:** Psalm 18; Psalm 144
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 18:1–19 — God Came Down to Rescue
     - In distress he called on the LORD; the earth shook, God “rode upon a cherub” and came down, and He “drew me out of many waters” (18:2, 18:6–7, 18:10, 18:16).
  2. Psalm 18:20–30 — Merciful to the Merciful
     - The LORD rewarded him for the cleanness of his hands; “With the merciful thou wilt shew thyself merciful”, and “As for God, his way is perfect” (18:20, 18:25, 18:30).
  3. Psalm 18:31–50 — Strength for the Battle
     - “For who is God save the LORD?” He gives strength for war and victory over enemies, and “Great deliverance giveth he to his king” (18:31–32, 18:34, 18:50).
  4. Psalm 144 — The People Whose God Is the LORD
     - “Blessed be the LORD my strength, which teacheth my hands to war”; man is like a passing shadow, yet God delivers, and “happy is that people, whose God is the LORD” (144:1, 144:4, 144:10, 144:15).
- **Split option:** None. Psalm 144 alone would be a 1-lesson flavor, and Psalm 18 is one poem (2.4).
- **People (2):** God, David
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** The flavor’s three names come from the text: “The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer” (18:2).

### 13. Psalms: From Scorn to Praise
- **Range:** Psalm 22; Psalm 31; Psalm 71
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 22:1–21 — Mocked and Surrounded
     - “My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?”: those who see him laugh him to scorn, bulls and dogs surround him, and “They part my garments among them” (22:1, 22:7, 22:12, 22:16, 22:18).
  2. Psalm 22:22–31 — All the Ends of the World Turn
     - He turns to praise in the congregation, for God “hath not despised nor abhorred the affliction of the afflicted”, and “All the ends of the world shall remember and turn unto the LORD” (22:22, 22:24, 22:27).
  3. Psalm 31 — Times in God’s Hand
     - A reproach to his neighbors and “like a broken vessel”, the speaker says, “Into thine hand I commit my spirit” and “My times are in thy hand” (31:5, 31:11–12, 31:15).
  4. Psalm 71 — Praise from Youth to Old Age
     - Trusting God from birth, the speaker asks, “Cast me not off in the time of old age”, while enemies say God has forsaken him; he will praise God “more and more” (71:6, 71:9, 71:11, 71:14).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 31 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (2):** God, Jacob (Israel)
- **Places (1):** Bashan
- **Turn:** Each poem starts in scorn: “All they that see me laugh me to scorn” (22:7).

### 14. Psalms: Confessing Sin to God
- **Range:** Psalm 25; Psalm 32; Psalm 38; Psalm 51
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 25 — Remember Not the Sins of Youth
     - The speaker lifts up his soul to the LORD and asks, “Shew me thy ways”, “Remember not the sins of my youth”, and “pardon mine iniquity; for it is great” (25:1, 25:4, 25:7, 25:11).
  2. Psalm 32 — The Joy of Being Forgiven
     - “Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven”: while he kept silent his bones wasted away, but when he confessed, “thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin” (32:1, 32:3, 32:5).
  3. Psalm 38 — Sin Too Heavy to Carry
     - His iniquities have gone over his head “as an heavy burden”; sick and left by his friends, he says, “I will declare mine iniquity; I will be sorry for my sin” (38:4, 38:11, 38:18).
  4. Psalm 51 — Washed Whiter Than Snow
     - “Have mercy upon me, O God”: “wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow”, “Create in me a clean heart”, for God will not despise “a broken and a contrite heart” (51:1, 51:7, 51:10, 51:17).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 38 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (2):** God, Holy Spirit
- **Places (2):** Jerusalem, Zion
- **Turn:** The poems confess: “I acknowledged my sin unto thee” (32:5).

### 15. Psalms: Wait on the LORD
- **Range:** Psalm 27; Psalm 40; Psalm 70; Psalm 62
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Psalm 27 — The One Thing Desired
     - “The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear?”; the one thing he seeks is to dwell in the LORD’s house, and he ends, “Wait on the LORD: be of good courage” (27:1, 27:4, 27:14).
  2. Psalm 40; Psalm 70 — Lifted Out of the Miry Clay
     - “I waited patiently for the LORD”, who lifted him out of “the miry clay” and put “a new song in my mouth”; the short poem after it repeats his closing cry, “Make haste” (40:1–3, 40:13; 70:1).
  3. Psalm 62 — Waiting for God Alone
     - “Truly my soul waiteth upon God”, for “He only is my rock”; men are lighter than vanity, so “if riches increase, set not your heart upon them” (62:1–2, 62:9–10).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (1):** God
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** The poems wait: “Wait on the LORD” (27:14); “I waited patiently for the LORD” (40:1).

### 16. Psalms: The LORD Reigns
- **Range:** Psalm 29; Psalm 47; Psalm 93; Psalms 95–100
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Psalm 29; Psalm 47; Psalm 93 — King Over All the Earth
     - The voice of the LORD breaks the cedars, and “the LORD sitteth King for ever”; “God is the King of all the earth”; and “The LORD reigneth”, mightier than the waves of the sea (29:5, 29:10; 47:7; 93:1, 93:4).
  2. Psalm 95 — Do Not Harden Your Hearts
     - “O come, let us worship and bow down”, for He is a great King and His people are “the sheep of his hand”; “Harden not your heart” as in the wilderness (95:3, 95:6–8).
  3. Psalm 96 — Sing the LORD a New Song
     - “O sing unto the LORD a new song”, for “all the gods of the nations are idols”; tell the nations “that the LORD reigneth”, for “he cometh to judge the earth” (96:1, 96:5, 96:10, 96:13).
  4. Psalms 97–98 — Joy Before the LORD the King
     - “The LORD reigneth; let the earth rejoice”: the hills melt like wax before Him, and “Ye that love the LORD, hate evil”; His right hand has won the victory, so “Let the floods clap their hands” (97:1, 97:5, 97:10; 98:1, 98:8).
  5. Psalms 99–100 — Enter His Gates with Thanks
     - The LORD reigns and is holy; Moses, Aaron, and Samuel called on Him and He answered; “Enter into his gates with thanksgiving”, for “his mercy is everlasting” (99:1, 99:5–6; 100:4–5).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 96 (2 + 3 lessons).
- **People (6):** God, Jacob (Israel), Aaron, Abraham, Moses, Samuel
- **Places (4):** Lebanon, Zion, Kadesh, Sirion
- **Turn:** Three poems open with the same words: “The LORD reigneth” (93:1; 97:1; 99:1).

### 17. Psalms: When the Wicked Prosper
- **Range:** Psalm 37; Psalm 73
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 37:1–11 — Do Not Fret Over Evildoers
     - “Fret not thyself because of evildoers”: trust in the LORD, “Delight thyself also in the LORD”, commit your way to Him, and “the meek shall inherit the earth” (37:1, 37:3–5, 37:11).
  2. Psalm 37:12–22 — A Little Is Better Than Riches
     - The wicked plot and draw the sword, but their swords enter their own hearts; “A little that a righteous man hath is better than the riches of many wicked” (37:12, 37:14–16).
  3. Psalm 37:23–40 — The Righteous Never Forsaken

- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Lamentations 1 — No Sorrow Like Zion’s Sorrow
     - “How doth the city sit solitary, that was full of people!”: Judah has gone into captivity because “Jerusalem hath grievously sinned”, and Zion cries, “behold, and see if there be any sorrow like unto my sorrow” (1:1, 1:3, 1:8, 1:12).
