How to read an entry:
- **Title** — after the book name, what the player learns from the whole flavor, as this recipe teaches and tests it (2.10).
- **Range** — the verses the flavor teaches and reveals (Question Rules 2.12 format).
- **Parallel** — other accounts of the same events: revealed after the base, never a question source (2.17).
- **Frame** — verses outside the range that lesson 1 also draws on (Letters: who wrote it, to whom, and why, 7.L2).
- **Lessons** — one numbered line per lesson: its range, then its title in plain words (2.10). The line under it says what the lesson teaches toward the flavor title.
- **Split option** — where the flavor could become two flavors that each still have 2–5 lessons (2.4), or "None".
- **People / Places** — who and where the reference layer links to the range, most-mentioned first.
- **Turn** — the words in the text that tie the flavor’s poems together, or where a long poem’s section begins.

## Psalms

### 1. Psalms: The Life God Blesses
- **Range:** Psalm 1; Psalm 112; Psalm 15; Psalm 24; Psalm 26; Psalm 34
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Psalm 1; Psalm 112 — Psalm 1; Psalm 112
     - The blessed man delights in the LORD’s law and is “like a tree planted by the rivers of water”, while the ungodly are chaff; in the next poem, “Blessed is the man that feareth the LORD”: “he is gracious, and full of compassion, and righteous”, and “he hath given to the poor” (1:2–4; 112:1, 112:4, 112:9).
  2. Psalm 15; Psalm 24; Psalm 26 — Psalm 15; Psalm 24; Psalm 26
     - The one who may dwell on God’s holy hill “speaketh the truth in his heart”; the one who may stand in His holy place has “clean hands, and a pure heart”, and the gates lift up their heads for “the King of glory”; the third speaker has walked in integrity and will “wash mine hands in innocency”, and asks, “Gather not my soul with sinners” (15:1–2; 24:3–4, 24:7; 26:1, 26:6, 26:9).
  3. Psalm 34 — Taste and See That God Is Good
     - A poor man cried and the LORD saved him; whoever wants long life keeps his tongue from evil and will “seek peace, and pursue it”, for “The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart” (34:6, 34:8, 34:12–14, 34:18).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (2):** God, Jacob (Israel)
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** Each poem shows the person God blesses: “Blessed is the man” (1:1; 112:1); “He shall receive the blessing from the LORD” (24:5).
- **Notes:** In Psalm 112 the one who is “gracious, and full of compassion” is the man who fears the LORD (112:1, 112:4), so it is taught here, not with the poems about the LORD’s own grace.

### 2. Psalms: God’s Chosen King
- **Range:** Psalm 2; Psalms 20–21; Psalm 45; Psalm 72; Psalm 101; Psalm 110
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Psalm 2 — Kings Who Rage Against God
     - The kings of the earth plot “against the LORD, and against his anointed”, but He laughs, sets His king on Zion, and warns them to “Serve the LORD with fear” (2:2, 2:4, 2:6, 2:11).
  2. Psalms 20–21 — Psalms 20–21
     - The people pray for their king in the day of trouble, saying “we will remember the name of the LORD our God”; then the king rejoices in God’s strength, because “the king trusteth in the LORD” (20:1, 20:7, 21:1, 21:7).
  3. Psalm 45 — A Song About the King
     - The speaker tells of “the things which I have made touching the king”: he is “fairer than the children of men” and rides out for truth and meekness, and the king’s daughter is brought to him “in raiment of needlework” (45:1–2, 45:4, 45:13–14).
  4. Psalm 72 — Ruling from Sea to Sea
     - A prayer that the king and the king’s son rule with God’s righteousness: “He shall judge the poor of the people”, save the needy, and “have dominion also from sea to sea”, and all nations shall serve him (72:1, 72:4, 72:8, 72:11–13).
  5. Psalm 101; Psalm 110 — Psalm 101; Psalm 110
     - One vows to walk “with a perfect heart” and cut off wicked doers from the city of the LORD; then the LORD says to another, “Sit thou at my right hand”, and swears, “Thou art a priest for ever” (101:2, 101:8; 110:1–2, 110:4).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 45 (2 + 3 lessons).
- **People (6):** God, Jesus Christ, David, Jacob (Israel), Jesse, Melchisedec
- **Places (7):** Zion, Sheba, Lebanon, Ophir, Seba, Tarshish, Tyre
- **Turn:** God names the king His own: “Yet have I set my king upon my holy hill of Zion” (2:6).

### 3. Psalms: Praying Morning and Night
- **Range:** Psalms 3–5; Psalm 59; Psalm 92; Psalm 143
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Psalms 3–4 — Psalms 3–4
     - Many say there is no help for him in God, yet the speaker lies down and sleeps, “for the LORD sustained me”; in the second poem, “I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep” (3:2, 3:5; 4:8).
  2. Psalm 5 — God Has No Pleasure in Evil
     - The speaker says, “in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee”, and asks, “make thy way straight before my face”, because God takes no pleasure in wickedness and will bless the righteous (5:3–4, 5:8, 5:12).
  3. Psalm 59 — Enemies Who Return at Evening
     - Enemies come back each evening, making “a noise like a dog” around the city, but the speaker will “sing aloud of thy mercy in the morning” (59:6, 59:14, 59:16).
  4. Psalm 92 — Flourishing Like a Palm Tree
     - It is good to give thanks, showing God’s “lovingkindness in the morning, and thy faithfulness every night”; the wicked spring up like grass, but “The righteous shall flourish like the palm tree” (92:1–2, 92:7, 92:12).
  5. Psalm 143 — Led by God’s Good Spirit
     - Crushed by the enemy, the speaker stretches forth his hands, and his soul thirsts “as a thirsty land” and asks, “Cause me to hear thy lovingkindness in the morning”, and “Teach me to do thy will” (143:3, 143:6, 143:8, 143:10).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 59 (2 + 3 lessons).
- **People (2):** God, Jacob (Israel)
- **Places (1):** Lebanon
- **Turn:** The poems pray at the turn of the day: “I laid me down and slept; I awaked; for the LORD sustained me” (3:5).

### 4. Psalms: When Death Comes Close
- **Range:** Psalm 6; Psalm 30; Psalm 41; Psalm 88; Psalm 116
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 6; Psalm 30 — Psalm 6; Psalm 30
     - Weak and grieving, the speaker floods his bed with tears and asks, “in the grave who shall give thee thanks?”, then knows “the LORD hath heard the voice of my weeping”; in the next poem the LORD brought his soul up from the grave, “weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning”, and his mourning is turned into dancing (6:2, 6:5–6, 6:8; 30:3, 30:5, 30:11).
  2. Psalm 41 — Kept Alive Through Sickness
     - The LORD will strengthen the one who considers the poor “upon the bed of languishing”; enemies say he will not rise, and even “mine own familiar friend” has turned against him (41:1, 41:3, 41:8–9).
  3. Psalm 88 — Counted Among the Dead
     - The speaker has cried “day and night”, his life draws near the grave, and he asks, “shall the dead arise and praise thee?”; the poem ends still in darkness (88:1, 88:3, 88:10, 88:18).
  4. Psalm 116 — Keeping Promises to the LORD
     - “The sorrows of death compassed me”, but the LORD delivered his soul from death, so he asks, “What shall I render unto the LORD”, and says, “I will pay my vows unto the LORD now in the presence of all his people” (116:3, 116:8, 116:12, 116:14).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 88 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (1):** God
- **Places (1):** Jerusalem
- **Turn:** The poems face the grave: “For in death there is no remembrance of thee” (6:5).

### 5. Psalms: Hated Without a Cause
- **Range:** Psalm 7; Psalm 35; Psalm 69; Psalm 109
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Psalm 7 — The Pit the Wicked Dug
     - Chased by one who would tear him like a lion, the speaker asks God to judge him by his integrity; the wicked man “made a pit, and digged it, and is fallen into the ditch which he made” (7:2, 7:8, 7:15).
  2. Psalm 35 — Repaid Evil for Good
     - False witnesses rose up and “rewarded me evil for good”, though he mourned for them when they were sick; he asks the LORD to plead his cause (35:1, 35:11–14).
  3. Psalm 69:1–21 — Sinking in Deep Waters
     - The waters have come in to his soul; those who “hate me without a cause” outnumber the hairs of his head, and in his thirst “they gave me vinegar to drink” (69:1–2, 69:4, 69:21).
  4. Psalm 69:22–36 — The LORD Hears the Poor
     - He asks God to repay his enemies, “Let their table become a snare”, then turns to praise, for “the LORD heareth the poor” and will save Zion (69:22, 69:30, 69:33, 69:35).
  5. Psalm 109 — Cursed by Men, Blessed by God
     - Liars repay his love with hatred; he asks that the cursing they loved come back on them, and trusts that the LORD “shall stand at the right hand of the poor” (109:2–5, 109:17, 109:28, 109:31).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 69 (2 + 3 lessons).
- **People (4):** God, Jacob (Israel), Judah (patriarch), Satan
- **Places (1):** Zion
- **Turn:** The same words recur: “hate me without a cause” (69:4); “fought against me without a cause” (109:3).

### 6. Psalms: Creation Shows God’s Glory
- **Range:** Psalm 8; Psalm 19; Psalm 33; Psalm 104
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 8; Psalm 19 — Psalm 8; Psalm 19
     - Looking at the moon and stars, the speaker asks, “What is man, that thou art mindful of him?”; “The heavens declare the glory of God”, and “The law of the LORD is perfect” (8:3–4; 19:1, 19:7).
  2. Psalm 33 — He Spoke and It Was Done
     - “By the word of the LORD were the heavens made”; “he spake, and it was done”, and no king is saved by an army, but the LORD’s eye is on those who fear Him (33:6, 33:9, 33:16, 33:18).
  3. Psalm 104:1–18 — Waters Given Their Borders
     - God covers Himself with light and stretches out the heavens; He set the waters a bound they may not pass, sends springs into the valleys, and makes grass grow for the cattle (104:2, 104:9–10, 104:14).
  4. Psalm 104:19–35 — God Sends Out His Spirit
     - The moon marks the seasons, young lions seek their meat from God, the sea teems with creatures, and “These wait all upon thee”; when He sends His spirit, “they are created” (104:19, 104:21, 104:25, 104:27, 104:30).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 104 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (1):** God
- **Places (1):** Lebanon
- **Turn:** The poems open on creation: “O LORD our Lord, how excellent is thy name in all the earth!” (8:1).

### 7. Psalms: God Is the Judge
- **Range:** Psalm 9; Psalm 50; Psalm 58; Psalm 75; Psalms 82–83
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 9 — A Refuge for the Oppressed
     - The LORD sits on His throne judging right; He “will be a refuge for the oppressed”, and the nations sink into the pit they made (9:4, 9:9, 9:15).
  2. Psalm 50 — Offer God Thanksgiving
     - God calls heaven and earth to judge His people; He needs no bulls, for “the cattle upon a thousand hills” are His, so “Offer unto God thanksgiving” (50:4, 50:10, 50:14, 50:23).
  3. Psalm 58; Psalm 75; Psalm 82 — Psalm 58; Psalm 75; Psalm 82
     - Those asked, “do ye judge uprightly, O ye sons of men?” are like “the deaf adder”, yet “verily he is a God that judgeth in the earth”; “God is the judge: he putteth down one, and setteth up another”; and God asks those who judge unjustly, “How long”, and tells them, “Defend the poor and fatherless” (58:1, 58:4, 58:11; 75:7; 82:2–3).
  4. Psalm 83 — Most High Over All the Earth
     - Nations plot against Israel: “Come, and let us cut them off from being a nation”; the speaker asks, “Keep not thou silence, O God”, and asks God to do to them “as to Sisera”, “That men may know” He is most high over all the earth (83:1, 83:4, 83:9, 83:18).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 58 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (13):** God, Asaph (Levite), Amalek, Ben-ammi, Jabin (king of Hazor), Jacob (Israel), Lot, Moab, Oreb, Sisera (army captain), Zalmunna, Zebah, Zeeb
- **Places (8):** Zion, Asshur, Edom, En-dor, Gebal, Kishon, Midian, Tyre
- **Turn:** The poems name God as judge: “for God is judge himself” (50:6); “But God is the judge” (75:7).

### 8. Psalms: Nothing Is Hidden from God
- **Range:** Psalm 10; Psalm 14; Psalm 53; Psalm 94; Psalm 139
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Psalm 10; Psalm 14; Psalm 53 — Psalm 10; Psalm 14; Psalm 53
     - The proud wicked hunt the poor and say in their hearts, “God hath forgotten: he hideth his face”, but “Thou hast seen it”, and “thou art the helper of the fatherless”; then two nearly matching poems: “The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God”, and God looks down from heaven and finds “none that doeth good, no, not one” (10:2, 10:11, 10:14; 14:1–3; 53:1–3).
  2. Psalm 94 — He Who Made the Eye Sees
     - The wicked say, “The LORD shall not see”, but “he that formed the eye, shall he not see?”; when the speaker’s foot slipped, “thy mercy, O LORD, held me up” (94:7, 94:9, 94:18).
  3. Psalm 139 — Known and Searched by God
     - The LORD knows his sitting down and rising up and every word on his tongue; there is nowhere to flee from His presence, for “the darkness hideth not from thee”, and he ends, “Search me, O God” (139:1–4, 139:7, 139:12, 139:23).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (2):** God, Jacob (Israel)
- **Places (1):** Zion
- **Turn:** The wicked speak in their hearts: “He hath said in his heart, God hath forgotten” (10:11); “The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God” (14:1).

### 9. Psalms: Putting Trust in the LORD
- **Range:** Psalm 11; Psalm 13; Psalm 16; Psalm 23; Psalm 28; Psalm 54; Psalm 56; Psalm 55; Psalms 141–142
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Psalm 11; Psalm 13 — Psalm 11; Psalm 13
     - Told to “Flee as a bird to your mountain”, the speaker answers that “The LORD is in his holy temple”; in the next poem he asks, “How long wilt thou forget me”, yet ends, “I have trusted in thy mercy” (11:1, 11:4; 13:1, 13:5).
  2. Psalm 16; Psalm 23 — Psalm 16; Psalm 23
     - “The LORD is the portion of mine inheritance and of my cup”, who shows “the path of life”; and “The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want”, even in “the valley of the shadow of death” (16:5, 16:11; 23:1, 23:4).
  3. Psalm 28; Psalm 54; Psalm 56 — Psalm 28; Psalm 54; Psalm 56
     - The speaker cries to the LORD his rock, and then says, “The LORD is my strength and my shield; my heart trusted in him, and I am helped”; in the next poem strangers rise against him, but “God is mine helper”; in the third enemies twist his words every day, but “What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee”, and he asks God, “put thou my tears into thy bottle” (28:1, 28:7; 54:3–4; 56:3, 56:5, 56:8).
  4. Psalm 55 — Give Your Burden to the LORD
     - The one who turned on the speaker was no enemy but “a man mine equal, my guide, and mine acquaintance”; he wishes for “wings like a dove” to fly away, then says, “Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee” (55:6, 55:12–13, 55:22).
  5. Psalms 141–142 — Psalms 141–142
     - He prays, “Set a watch, O LORD, before my mouth”, and asks to be kept from evil men; then, when “no man cared for my soul”, he cries, “Thou art my refuge” (141:3–4; 142:4–5).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 54 (2 + 3 lessons).
- **People (2):** God, Jesus Christ
- **Places (1):** Gath
- **Turn:** The poems choose trust: “In the LORD put I my trust” (11:1); “in thee do I put my trust” (16:1); “my heart trusted in him” (28:7).
- **Notes:** Psalms 23, 54, and 142 do not say “trust”, and no other flavor fits them more clearly by the text, so each stays beside a trust poem it shares words with: Psalm 23 speaks of “my cup” and “the paths of righteousness” as Psalm 16 speaks of “my cup” and “the path of life” (16:5, 16:11; 23:3, 23:5); Psalm 54’s “God is mine helper” answers “I am helped” (54:4; 28:7); and Psalm 142 has the same laid snare as Psalm 141 (141:9; 142:3).

### 11. Psalms: Shelter Under God’s Wings
- **Range:** Psalm 17; Psalm 36; Psalm 57; Psalm 61; Psalm 91
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 17 — A Prayer from Honest Lips
     - Asking God to hear a prayer from honest lips, the speaker says, “Keep me as the apple of the eye, hide me under the shadow of thy wings”, safe from enemies like a greedy lion (17:1, 17:8, 17:12).
  2. Psalm 36 — The Fountain of Life
     - The wicked man has “no fear of God before his eyes”, but God’s mercy reaches the heavens, people trust “under the shadow of thy wings”, and “with thee is the fountain of life” (36:1, 36:5, 36:7, 36:9).
  3. Psalm 57; Psalm 61 — Psalm 57; Psalm 61
     - Among lions, the speaker takes refuge “in the shadow of thy wings” until trouble passes, and his heart is fixed to sing; in the next poem he asks, “lead me to the rock that is higher than I” (57:1, 57:4, 57:7; 61:2, 61:4).
  4. Psalm 91 — Safe in the Secret Place
     - Whoever dwells “in the secret place of the most High” is covered with His feathers; a thousand may fall at his side, and God promises, “I will be with him in trouble” (91:1, 91:4, 91:7, 91:15).

- **People (1):** God
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** The poems share one picture: “hide me under the shadow of thy wings” (17:8).

### 12. Psalms: The LORD Our Rock
- **Range:** Psalm 18; Psalm 144
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Psalm 18:1–19 — God Came Down to Rescue
     - In distress he called on the LORD; the earth shook, God “rode upon a cherub” and came down, and He “drew me out of many waters” (18:2, 18:6–7, 18:10, 18:16).
  2. Psalm 18:20–30 — Merciful to the Merciful
     - The LORD rewarded him for the cleanness of his hands; “With the merciful thou wilt shew thyself merciful”, and “As for God, his way is perfect” (18:20, 18:25, 18:30).
  3. Psalm 18:31–50 — Strength for the Battle
     - “For who is God save the LORD?” He gives strength for war and victory over enemies, and “Great deliverance giveth he to his king” (18:31–32, 18:34, 18:50).
  4. Psalm 144 — The People Whose God Is the LORD
     - “Blessed be the LORD my strength, which teacheth my hands to war”; man is like a passing shadow, yet God delivers, and “happy is that people, whose God is the LORD” (144:1, 144:4, 144:10, 144:15).
- **Split option:** None. Psalm 144 alone would be a 1-lesson flavor, and Psalm 18 is one poem (2.4).
- **People (2):** God, David
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** The flavor’s three names come from the text: “The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer” (18:2).

### 13. Psalms: From Scorn to Praise
- **Range:** Psalm 22; Psalm 31; Psalm 71
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Psalm 22 — Forsaken, Then Heard by God
     - “My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?”: those who see him laugh him to scorn, “strong bulls of Bashan” and dogs surround him, and “They part my garments among them”; then he turns to praise in the congregation, for God “hath not despised nor abhorred the affliction of the afflicted”, and “All the ends of the world shall remember and turn unto the LORD” (22:1, 22:7, 22:12, 22:16, 22:18, 22:22, 22:24, 22:27).
  2. Psalm 31 — Putting His Life in God’s Hand
     - A reproach to his neighbors and “like a broken vessel”, the speaker says, “Into thine hand I commit my spirit” and “My times are in thy hand” (31:5, 31:11–12, 31:15).
  3. Psalm 71 — Trusting God into Old Age
     - Trusting God from birth, the speaker asks, “Cast me not off in the time of old age”, while enemies say God has forsaken him; he will praise God “more and more” (71:6, 71:9, 71:11, 71:14).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (2):** God, Jacob (Israel)
- **Places (1):** Bashan
- **Turn:** Psalm 22 opens in scorn, “All they that see me laugh me to scorn” (22:7); Psalms 31 and 71 open with the same words, “In thee, O LORD, do I put my trust” (31:1; 71:1), and all three end in praise.

### 14. Psalms: Confessing Sin to God
- **Range:** Psalm 25; Psalm 32; Psalm 38; Psalm 51
- **Lessons:** 4

  3. Psalm 38 — Sin Too Heavy to Carry
     - His iniquities have gone over his head “as an heavy burden”; sick and left by his friends, he says, “I will declare mine iniquity; I will be sorry for my sin” (38:4, 38:11, 38:18).
  4. Psalm 51 — Washed Whiter Than Snow
     - “Have mercy upon me, O God”: “wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow”, “Create in me a clean heart”, for God will not despise “a broken and a contrite heart” (51:1, 51:7, 51:10, 51:17).
- **Split option:** At Psalm 38 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (2):** God, Holy Spirit
- **Places (2):** Jerusalem, Zion
- **Turn:** The poems confess: “I acknowledged my sin unto thee” (32:5).

### 16. Psalms: The LORD Reigns
- **Range:** Psalm 29; Psalm 47; Psalm 93; Psalms 95–100
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Psalm 29; Psalm 47; Psalm 93 — Psalm 29; Psalm 47; Psalm 93

  2. Psalm 95 — Do Not Harden Your Heart

- **Notes:** Staging holds a heading for only one of these poems: Psalm 132, “A Song of degrees.” If the other psalm headings are loaded, check whether the rest of this run carries the same heading; the intro could then name it.

### 33. Psalms: Let Everything Praise the LORD
- **Range:** Psalm 113; Psalms 146–150

  1. Psalm 113; Psalm 146 — Psalm 113; Psalm 146

  2. Psalm 147 — He Heals the Brokenhearted

## Song of Solomon

### 34. Song of Solomon: The Bride’s Beloved
- **Range:** Song of Solomon 1:1–5:1
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Song of Solomon 1:1–2:7 — Like a Lily Among Thorns
     - The song “which is Solomon’s” opens with a love that “is better than wine”; she is “As the lily among thorns”, he is like an apple tree, and “his banner over me was love” (1:1–2, 2:2–4).
  2. Song of Solomon 2:8–3:5 — Rise Up and Come Away
     - Her beloved comes leaping over the hills and calls, “Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away”, for the winter is past; by night she seeks “him whom my soul loveth” and finds him (2:8, 2:10–11, 3:1, 3:4).
  3. Song of Solomon 3:6–5:1 — King Solomon’s Wedding Day
     - King Solomon comes in his chariot, crowned “in the day of his espousals”; he tells his spouse, “Thou art all fair, my love; there is no spot in thee”, and calls her “A garden inclosed” (3:9, 3:11, 4:7–8, 4:12, 5:1).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (4):** Solomon, David, Kedar, Pharaoh (of the Exodus)
- **Places (10):** Jerusalem, Lebanon, Amana, Engedi, Gilead, Hermon, Kedar, Saron, Senir, Zion
- **Turn:** The book names itself: “The song of songs, which is Solomon’s” (1:1).

### 35. Song of Solomon: Love Strong as Death
- **Range:** Song of Solomon 5:2–8:14
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Song of Solomon 5:2–6:3 — Altogether Lovely
     - She is slow to open the door, and when she does, “my beloved had withdrawn himself”; the watchmen strike her, and she tells the daughters of Jerusalem her beloved is “the chiefest among ten thousand” and “altogether lovely” (5:3, 5:6–8, 5:10, 5:16).
  2. Song of Solomon 6:4–7:9 — She Is His One and Only
     - He calls her “beautiful, O my love, as Tirzah” and says, “My dove, my undefiled is but one”, and the daughters “saw her, and blessed her”; she is called “O Shulamite”, and he praises her from her feet to her head (6:4, 6:9, 6:13, 7:1–5).
  3. Song of Solomon 7:10–8:14 — Love That Cannot Be Bought
     - She says, “I am my beloved’s, and his desire is toward me”, and asks him to go out into the field with her; “Set me as a seal upon thine heart”, for “love is strong as death”; “Many waters cannot quench love”, and if a man gave “all the substance of his house for love, it would utterly be contemned” (7:10–11, 8:6–7).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (1):** Solomon
- **Places (9):** Jerusalem, Lebanon, Baal-hamon, Bath-rabbim, Carmel, Damascus, Gilead, Heshbon, Tirzah
- **Turn:** A new night begins: “I sleep, but my heart waketh: it is the voice of my beloved that knocketh” (5:2).

## Lamentations

### 36. Lamentations: Zion Left in Ruins
- **Range:** Lamentations 1–2; Lamentations 4–5
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Lamentations 1 — No Sorrow Like Zion’s Sorrow
     - “How doth the city sit solitary, that was full of people!”: Judah has gone into captivity because “Jerusalem hath grievously sinned”, and Zion cries, “behold, and see if there be any sorrow like unto my sorrow” (1:1, 1:3, 1:8, 1:12).
  2. Lamentations 2 — The Lord Became Like an Enemy
     - In His anger “The Lord was as an enemy”, throwing down Zion’s walls and casting off His altar; her prophets saw “vain and foolish things”, and the city is told to pour out its heart like water before the Lord (2:5, 2:7–8, 2:14, 2:19).
  3. Lamentations 4 — Children Begging for Bread
     - “How is the gold become dim!”: “the young children ask bread, and no man breaketh it unto them”, the LORD has poured out His fierce anger for the sins of the prophets and priests, and Zion is told her punishment “is accomplished” (4:1, 4:4, 4:11, 4:13, 4:22).
  4. Lamentations 5 — Asking God to Turn Them Back
     - “Remember, O LORD, what is come upon us”: their land is given to strangers and “The crown is fallen from our head”, yet “Thou, O LORD, remainest for ever”, and they pray, “Turn thou us unto thee, O LORD” (5:1–2, 5:16, 5:19, 5:21).
- **Split option:** At Lamentations 4 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (3):** God, Jacob (Israel), Esau (Edom)
- **Places (7):** Zion, Jerusalem, Edom, Egypt, Mount Zion, Sodom, Uz
- **Turn:** The poems mourn the same ruin: “all her gates are desolate” (1:4); “the mountain of Zion, which is desolate” (5:18).
- **Notes:** Chapter 3 speaks as one afflicted man and turns to hope, so it is its own flavor; chapters 1, 2, 4, and 5 all mourn the fallen city and are taught together here.

### 37. Lamentations: The Man Who Found Hope
- **Range:** Lamentations 3
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Lamentations 3:1–20 — Walled In by Darkness
     - “I am the man that hath seen affliction by the rod of his wrath”: led into darkness, hedged in, his prayer shut out, he says, “My strength and my hope is perished from the LORD” (3:1–2, 3:7–8, 3:18).
  2. Lamentations 3:21–39 — Mercies New Every Morning
     - “This I recall to my mind, therefore have I hope”: the LORD’s compassions fail not, “They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness”, and “the Lord will not cast off for ever” (3:21–23, 3:31).
  3. Lamentations 3:40–66 — Heard from the Dungeon
     - “Let us search and try our ways, and turn again to the LORD”; with eyes running down, the speaker called “out of the low dungeon”, and God drew near and said, “Fear not” (3:40, 3:48, 3:55, 3:57).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (1):** God
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** The poem opens: “I am the man that hath seen affliction” (3:1).
