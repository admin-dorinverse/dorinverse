How to read an entry:
- **Title** — after the book name, what the player learns from the whole flavor, as this recipe teaches and tests it (2.10).
- **Range** — the verses the flavor teaches and reveals (Question Rules 2.12 format).
- **Parallel** — other accounts of the same events: revealed after the base, never a question source (2.17).
- **Frame** — verses outside the range that lesson 1 also draws on (Letters: who wrote it, to whom, and why, 7.L2).
- **Lessons** — one numbered line per lesson: its range, then its title in plain words (2.10). The line under it says what the lesson teaches toward the flavor title.
- **Split option** — where the flavor could become two flavors that each still have 2–5 lessons (2.4), or "None".
- **People / Places** — who and where the reference layer links to the range, most-mentioned first.
- **Turn** — the words in the text where the section begins.

## Matthew

### 1. Matthew: Who Jesus Calls Blessed
- **Range:** Matthew 5:1–16
- **Parallel:** Luke 6:17–26
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Matthew 5:1–10 — The Meek, the Merciful, the Pure
     - On a mountain Jesus taught His disciples: “Blessed are the poor in spirit”, those that mourn, the meek, the merciful, “the pure in heart”, the peacemakers, and those “persecuted for righteousness’ sake” (5:1–5, 5:7–10).
  2. Matthew 5:11–16 — Salt and Light
     - Blessed are they whom men revile “for my sake”, “for great is your reward in heaven”; “Ye are the salt of the earth” and “Ye are the light of the world”, so “Let your light so shine before men” (5:11–14, 5:16).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (1):** God
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** The teaching opens: “And seeing the multitudes, he went up into a mountain” (5:1).
- **Notes:** Re-cut: 5:17–20 (the law fulfilled, and righteousness that must exceed the scribes’) moved to the next flavor, which it opens; lesson 2 now starts where the blessings turn to “ye” (5:11).

### 2. Matthew: Deeper Than the Old Sayings
- **Range:** Matthew 5:17–48
- **Parallel:** Luke 6:27–36
- **Frame:** Matthew 5:1–2
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Matthew 5:17–26 — Anger and the Law
     - “I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil” the law, and their righteousness must exceed the scribes’; they heard “Thou shalt not kill”, but anger toward a brother is in danger of judgment, so “first be reconciled to thy brother” (5:17, 5:20–22, 5:24).
  2. Matthew 5:27–37 — Lust, Divorce, and Oaths
     - Looking to lust is adultery “in his heart”; putting away a wife, except for fornication, causes her to commit adultery; and “Swear not at all”, but let your word be “Yea, yea; Nay, nay” (5:28, 5:31–32, 5:34, 5:37).
  3. Matthew 5:38–48 — Love Your Enemies
     - Not “An eye for an eye”, but “turn to him the other also”; and “Love your enemies”, for the Father sends rain “on the just and on the unjust” (5:38–39, 5:44–45).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (2):** God, Jesus Christ
- **Places (1):** Jerusalem
- **Turn:** The section opens: “Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets” (5:17); then each saying turns the same way: “Ye have heard that it was said by them of old time” (5:21).

### 6. Matthew: Sheep Among Wolves

  2. Matthew 10:16–31 — Taken Before Kings and Rulers

  3. Matthew 10:32–42 — Taking Up the Cross

### 7. Matthew: What the Kingdom Is Like
- **Range:** Matthew 13:1–30; Mark 4:26–29; Matthew 13:31–52
- **Parallel:** Mark 4:1–25; Mark 4:30–34; Luke 8:4–18
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Matthew 13:1–23 — Seed on Four Kinds of Ground
     - From a ship Jesus told of a sower whose seed fell by the way, on stony places, among thorns, and on good ground, then explained it: the good ground is he “that heareth the word, and understandeth it” (13:2–3, 13:8, 13:18, 13:23).
  2. Matthew 13:24–30; Mark 4:26–29; Matthew 13:31–43 — Wheat and Weeds Together
     - An enemy sowed tares among the wheat, and both grow “until the harvest”; Mark adds the seed that springs and grows while a man sleeps, “he knoweth not how”; the kingdom is also like mustard seed and leaven, and Jesus explained, “The field is the world” (Matthew 13:25, 13:30–31, 13:33, 13:38; Mark 4:27).
  3. Matthew 13:44–52 — The Pearl of Great Price
     - The kingdom is like treasure hid in a field and “one pearl of great price” that a man sells all to buy, and like a net, for the angels “sever the wicked from among the just” (13:44, 13:46–47, 13:49).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (3):** Jesus Christ, God, Isaiah
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** The block opens: “he spake many things unto them in parables” (13:3).
- **Notes:** Mark 4:26–29 (the seed that grows by itself) is a parable of this same day that Matthew does not tell, so it is taught here, in lesson 2 beside the other growing-seed parables, with a bridge naming Mark; it is no longer a parallel row. Mark 4:21–25 (the candle and the measure) and Luke 8:16–18 stay parallel rows.

### 9. Matthew: The Last Will Be First
- **Range:** Matthew 19:16–20:16
- **Parallel:** Mark 10:17–31; Luke 18:18–30
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Matthew 19:16–30 — Sell All and Follow Jesus
     - A young man asked, “what good thing shall I do, that I may have eternal life?”; told to sell all and follow, “he went away sorrowful: for he had great possessions”; it is easier “for a camel to go through the eye of a needle”; Peter asked what those who “have forsaken all” will have, and Jesus answered, “many that are first shall be last; and the last shall be first” (19:16, 19:21–22, 19:24, 19:27–30).
  2. Matthew 20:1–16 — Paid the Same for One Hour
     - Jesus tells of a householder who hired labourers all day and paid each “a penny”; the first murmured, and he answered, “Is thine eye evil, because I am good?”, for “the last shall be first, and the first last” (20:1–2, 20:9–12, 20:15–16).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (3):** Jesus Christ, God, Simon Peter
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** A question opens it: “Good Master, what good thing shall I do, that I may have eternal life?” (19:16).
- **Notes:** Re-cut: the labourers in the vineyard (19:27–20:16) used to share a flavor with Matthew 21:23–22:14, which the text does not set with it, and it is too short to stand alone. Peter’s question (19:27) answers what Jesus told the rich young man, and the same saying closes both parts (19:30, 20:16), so the flavor now starts where that scene starts (19:16).

  3. Matthew 22:1–14 — Guests Who Would Not Come
     - A king “made a marriage for his son”, but those bidden “made light of it”; his servants gathered “both bad and good” from the highways, a guest without “a wedding garment” was cast out, “For many are called, but few are chosen” (22:2, 22:5, 22:10–12, 22:14).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (3):** Jesus Christ, God, John the Baptist
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** In the temple the chief priests and elders ask: “By what authority doest thou these things?” (21:23).
- **Notes:** Re-cut: these three parables, told in the temple to the chief priests and elders, now stand as their own flavor; the labourers in the vineyard (Matthew 20:1–16) moved to "Matthew: The Last Will Be First." The title comes from 21:43: “The kingdom of God shall be taken from you, and given to a nation bringing forth the fruits thereof.”

## Luke

### 14. Luke: A Friend of Sinners
- **Range:** Luke 7:18–50
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Luke 7:18–35 — What Jesus Said About John
     - John’s disciples asked, “Art thou he that should come?”; Jesus told them what they had seen, called John “much more than a prophet”, and said people called Him “a friend of publicans and sinners” (7:19, 7:22, 7:26, 7:34).
  2. Luke 7:36–50 — The Woman Who Loved Much
     - At a Pharisee’s table a sinful woman washed Jesus’ feet with tears; He told Simon of two debtors both forgiven, asked which “will love him most”, and said “her sins, which are many, are forgiven; for she loved much” (7:37–38, 7:41–42, 7:47).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (4):** John the Baptist, Jesus Christ, God, Simon
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** The section opens: “And the disciples of John shewed him of all these things” (7:18).
- **Notes:** The parable of the two debtors (7:41–43) is taught with its scene (7:36–50). The scene answers the charge Jesus has just repeated, that He is “a friend of publicans and sinners” (7:34), so the two parts sit together.

### 15. Luke: Jesus Answers Those Who Ask
- **Range:** Luke 10:25–11:13
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Luke 10:25–37 — The Samaritan Who Showed Mercy
     - A lawyer asked, “Master, what shall I do to inherit eternal life?”, answered from the law, love God “and thy neighbour as thyself”, then asked, “And who is my neighbour?” A priest and a Levite passed by a wounded man, but “a certain Samaritan” had compassion on him; the neighbour was “He that shewed mercy on him”, and Jesus said, “Go, and do thou likewise” (10:25, 10:27, 10:29, 10:31–33, 10:37).
  2. Luke 10:38–11:13 — Listening to Jesus, Asking God
     - Martha asked Jesus to send Mary to help, but Mary, who “sat at Jesus’ feet, and heard his word,” had “chosen that good part”; a disciple asked, “Lord, teach us to pray”; Jesus gave a prayer and told of a friend who gives bread at midnight “because of his importunity”: “Ask, and it shall be given you” (10:39–42, 11:1–2, 11:5, 11:8–9).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (6):** Jesus Christ, God, Martha, Mary (sister of Lazarus), Holy Spirit, John the Baptist
- **Places (2):** Jericho, Jerusalem
- **Turn:** A question opens it: “Master, what shall I do to inherit eternal life?” (10:25).
- **Notes:** Re-cut: Luke 10:25–37 is now one lesson, since the lawyer’s question (10:25–29) was too short to stand alone and the parable answers its last question. Luke 11:1–13 used to share a flavor with Luke 18:1–14, which the text does not set with it, and it is too short to stand alone, so it joins the teaching just before it. Martha and Mary (10:38–42) sit between them and are taught in lesson 2, so the flavor runs straight through the text. In each scene someone asks Jesus something, and lesson 2 ends “every one that asketh receiveth” (11:10).
