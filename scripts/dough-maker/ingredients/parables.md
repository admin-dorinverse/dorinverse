How to read an entry:
- **Title** — after the book name, what the player learns from the whole flavor, as this recipe teaches and tests it (2.10).
- **Range** — the verses the flavor teaches and reveals (Question Rules 2.12 format).
- **Parallel** — other accounts of the same events: revealed after the base, never a question source (2.17).
- **Frame** — verses outside the range that lesson 1 also draws on (Letters: who wrote it, to whom, and why, 7.L2).
- **Lessons** — one numbered line per lesson: its range, then its title in plain words (2.10). The line under it says what the lesson teaches toward the flavor title.
- **Split option** — where the flavor could become two flavors that each still have 2–5 lessons (2.4), or "None".
- **People / Places** — who and where the reference layer links to the range, most-mentioned first.
- **Turn** — the words in the text where the section begins.

## Matthew

### 1. Matthew: Who Jesus Calls Blessed
- **Range:** Matthew 5:1–20
- **Parallel:** Luke 6:17–26
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Matthew 5:1–12 — The Blessed Ones
     - On a mountain Jesus taught His disciples: “Blessed are the poor in spirit”, the meek, the merciful, the peacemakers, and the persecuted, “for great is your reward in heaven” (5:1–3, 5:5, 5:7, 5:9, 5:12).
  2. Matthew 5:13–20 — Salt and Light
     - “Ye are the salt of the earth” and “Ye are the light of the world”; Jesus says of the law, “I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil”, and their righteousness must exceed the scribes’ (5:13–14, 5:17, 5:20).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (1):** God
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** The teaching opens: “And seeing the multitudes, he went up into a mountain” (5:1).

### 2. Matthew: Deeper Than the Old Sayings
- **Range:** Matthew 5:21–48
- **Parallel:** Luke 6:27–36
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Matthew 5:21–32 — Anger and Lust in the Heart
     - They heard “Thou shalt not kill”, but Jesus says anger toward a brother is in danger of judgment, so “first be reconciled to thy brother”; looking to lust is adultery “in his heart” (5:21–22, 5:24, 5:28).
  2. Matthew 5:33–48 — Love Your Enemies
     - Let your word be “Yea, yea; Nay, nay”; “turn to him the other also”; and “Love your enemies”, for the Father sends rain “on the just and on the unjust” (5:37, 5:39, 5:44–45).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (2):** God, Jesus Christ
- **Places (1):** Jerusalem
- **Turn:** Each saying turns the same way: “Ye have heard that it was said by them of old time” (5:21).

- **Lessons:** 3

  3. Matthew 13:44–52 — The Pearl of Great Price
     - The kingdom is like treasure hid in a field and “one pearl of great price” that a man sells all to buy, and like a net, for the angels “sever the wicked from among the just” (13:44, 13:46–47, 13:49).

### 9. Matthew: Many Called, Few Chosen
- **Range:** Matthew 19:27–20:16; Matthew 21:23–22:14
- **Parallel:** Mark 11:27–12:12; Luke 20:1–19
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Matthew 19:27–20:16 — Paid the Same for One Hour
     - Peter asks what those who left all will have; Jesus tells of a householder who hired labourers all day and paid each “a penny”; the first murmured, and he answered, “Is thine eye evil, because I am good?”, for “the last shall be first, and the first last” (19:27, 20:1–2, 20:9–12, 20:15–16).
  2. Matthew 21:23–32 — The Son Who Said No, Then Went
     - The chief priests and elders ask, “By what authority doest thou these things?”; Jesus asks about John’s baptism, then tells of two sons, the first of whom said “I will not” but went, and says “the publicans and the harlots go into the kingdom of God before you” (21:23, 21:25, 21:28–29, 21:31–32).
  3. Matthew 21:33–46 — Husbandmen Who Killed the Heir
     - A householder let out his vineyard to husbandmen who beat and killed his servants and then his son; the hearers say he “will miserably destroy those wicked men”, Jesus says “The kingdom of God shall be taken from you”, and the chief priests “perceived that he spake of them” (21:33, 21:35–41, 21:43, 21:45).
  4. Matthew 22:1–14 — Guests Who Would Not Come
     - A king “made a marriage for his son”, but those bidden “made light of it”; his servants gathered “both bad and good” from the highways, a guest without “a wedding garment” was cast out, “For many are called, but few are chosen” (22:2, 22:5, 22:10–12, 22:14).

- **People (4):** Jesus Christ, God, John the Baptist, Simon Peter
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** The vineyard parable opens: “For the kingdom of heaven is like unto a man that is an householder” (20:1).
