How to read an entry:
- **Title** — after the book name, what the player learns from the whole flavor, as this recipe teaches and tests it (2.10).
- **Range** — the verses the flavor teaches and reveals (Question Rules 2.12 format).
- **Parallel** — other accounts of the same events: revealed after the base, never a question source (2.17).
- **Frame** — verses outside the range that lesson 1 also draws on (Letters: who wrote it, to whom, and why, 7.L2).
- **Lessons** — one numbered line per lesson: its range, then its title in plain words (2.10). The line under it says what the lesson teaches toward the flavor title.
- **Split option** — where the flavor could become two flavors that each still have 2–5 lessons (2.4), or "None".
- **People / Places** — who and where the reference layer links to the range, most-mentioned first.
- **Turn** — the words in the text where the section begins.

## Genesis

### 1. Creation: God Makes All Things
- **Range:** Genesis 1–2
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Genesis 1:1–25 — From Light to Living Creatures
     - “In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth”; God says, “Let there be light: and there was light”, and day by day He makes sky, land, seas, plants, lights, and creatures, and sees “that it was good” (1:1, 1:3, 1:10, 1:16, 1:21, 1:25).
  2. Genesis 1:26–2:3 — Made in the Image of God
     - God makes man “in his own image”, male and female, and gives them dominion over the earth; He sees all He made is “very good”, and rests on the seventh day, which He blesses (1:27–28, 1:31, 2:2–3).
  3. Genesis 2:4–25 — A Garden and a Helper
     - The LORD God forms man “of the dust of the ground”, puts him in Eden, forbids “the tree of the knowledge of good and evil”, and makes a woman from his rib: “bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh” (2:7–8, 2:17, 2:22–23).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (3):** God, Adam, Holy Spirit
- **Places (8):** Eden, Assyria, Ethiopia, Euphrates, Gihon, Havilah (of Eden), Hiddekel, Pison
- **Turn:** The book opens: “In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth” (1:1).

### 2. The Fall: Sin Enters the Garden
- **Range:** Genesis 3
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Genesis 3:1–13 — The Serpent’s Question
     - The serpent asks, “Yea, hath God said”, and tells the woman, “Ye shall not surely die”; she and her husband eat, their eyes are opened, and they hide from the LORD God among the trees (3:1, 3:4, 3:6–8).
  2. Genesis 3:14–24 — Driven Out of Eden
     - The LORD curses the serpent and the ground, tells Adam, “dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return”, and sends him out of Eden, with Cherubims and “a flaming sword” guarding the tree of life (3:14, 3:17, 3:19, 3:23–24).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (3):** God, Adam, Eve
- **Places (1):** Eden
- **Turn:** The story turns: “Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field” (3:1).

### 3. Cain and Abel: The First Murder
- **Range:** Genesis 4
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Genesis 4:1–8 — Two Brothers, Two Offerings
     - Abel keeps sheep and Cain tills the ground; the LORD respects Abel’s offering and not Cain’s, warns that “sin lieth at the door”, and Cain rises up against his brother and slays him (4:2, 4:4–5, 4:7–8).
  2. Genesis 4:9–26 — A Wanderer on the Earth
     - Cain answers, “Am I my brother’s keeper?”, and becomes “a fugitive and a vagabond”, marked by the LORD; his line builds a city and handles the harp, and Eve bears Seth in place of Abel (4:9, 4:12, 4:15, 4:17, 4:21, 4:25).

### 4. Noah: Saved Through the Flood
- **Range:** Genesis 5:1–11:9
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Genesis 5:1–6:8 — Wickedness Fills the Earth
     - From Adam to Noah the generations live and die, though “Enoch walked with God: and he was not; for God took him”; the LORD sees that man’s wickedness is great, “But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD” (5:5, 5:24, 5:29, 6:5, 6:8).
  2. Genesis 6:9–7:24 — Into the Ark Before the Rain
     - God tells Noah, “Make thee an ark of gopher wood”; Noah, his family, and the animals two by two go in, “the LORD shut him in”, and the waters cover the mountains for a hundred and fifty days (6:14, 6:22, 7:9, 7:16, 7:19, 7:24).
  3. Genesis 8:1–22 — A Dove with an Olive Leaf
     - “God remembered Noah”; the ark rests on the mountains of Ararat, the dove returns with “an olive leaf pluckt off”, and after leaving the ark Noah builds an altar to the LORD (8:1, 8:4, 8:11, 8:18, 8:20).
  4. Genesis 9:1–29 — The Bow in the Cloud
     - God blesses Noah and promises no flood will again destroy the earth: “I do set my bow in the cloud”; later Ham sees his father’s nakedness, and Noah says, “Cursed be Canaan” (9:1, 9:11, 9:13, 9:22, 9:25).
  5. Genesis 10:1–11:9 — Scattered from Babel
     - The nations spread from the sons of Noah “after the flood”; men build “a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven”, and the LORD confounds their language and scatters them (10:1, 10:32, 11:4, 11:7–9).

### 6. Abram: Promised an Heir
- **Range:** Genesis 15:1–18:15
- **Lessons:** 4

  2. Genesis 16:1–16 — The God Who Sees Hagar
     - Sarai gives Hagar to Abram; Hagar conceives, is treated harshly, and flees, and the angel of the LORD finds her by a fountain and tells her to name her son Ishmael; she calls the LORD “Thou God seest me” (16:2, 16:6–7, 16:11, 16:13).

## Leviticus

### 28. Aaron: Made a Priest
- **Range:** Leviticus 1–10
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Leviticus 1–7 — The Offerings Explained
     - The LORD speaks to Moses “out of the tabernacle of the congregation” about burnt, meat, peace, sin, and trespass offerings, and commands, “The fire shall ever be burning upon the altar; it shall never go out” (1:1–3, 2:1, 3:1, 4:2–3, 6:13, 7:37–38).
  2. Leviticus 8 — Aaron Anointed
     - Moses washes Aaron and his sons with water, clothes Aaron, pours the anointing oil on his head, and they stay at the tabernacle door seven days (8:6–7, 8:12, 8:33).
  3. Leviticus 9 — The First Offerings Accepted
     - On the eighth day Aaron offers for himself and the people, “the glory of the LORD appeared unto all the people”, and fire from the LORD consumes the offering, and the people shout (9:1, 9:7, 9:23–24).
  4. Leviticus 10 — Strange Fire
     - Nadab and Abihu “offered strange fire before the LORD”, and fire devours them; Moses says God will be sanctified “in them that come nigh me”, “And Aaron held his peace” (10:1–3).

### 29. Moses: Laws for a Holy People
- **Range:** Leviticus 11–27
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Leviticus 11–15 — Clean and Unclean
     - God tells Israel which beasts they may eat, how a leper is judged by the priest, and how the unclean are made clean, for “ye shall be holy; for I am holy” (11:2, 11:44, 13:2–3, 13:45–46, 14:2).
  2. Leviticus 16 — The Day of Atonement
     - Given “after the death of the two sons of Aaron”, the law of the yearly atonement: Aaron enters the holy place, and the goat “shall bear upon him all their iniquities” into the wilderness (16:1–2, 16:21–22, 16:34).
  3. Leviticus 17–22 — Love Your Neighbor as Yourself
     - Laws for holy living: “the life of the flesh is in the blood”, “Ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy”, and “thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself” (17:11, 19:2, 19:18, 21:1).
  4. Leviticus 23–24 — The Feasts of the LORD
     - “These are the feasts of the LORD”, from the sabbath to the feast of tabernacles; then a man who “blasphemed the name of the LORD” is stoned, and the rule is given, “eye for eye, tooth for tooth” (23:3–4, 23:34, 24:11, 24:14, 24:20).
  5. Leviticus 25–27 — The Year of Liberty
     - In the fiftieth year Israel shall “proclaim liberty throughout all the land”; if they keep God’s statutes, “I will walk among you, and will be your God”, but if not, He will punish them (25:10, 26:3, 26:12, 26:14–16, 27:34).
- **Split option:** At Leviticus 17 (2 + 3 lessons).
- **People (11):** God, Jacob (Israel), Moses, Aaron, Molech, Canaan, Abraham, Dan, Dibri, Isaac, Shelomith
- **Places (5):** Egypt, Holy Place, Azazel, Canaan, Mount Sinai
- **Turn:** The holiness laws begin: “These are the beasts which ye shall eat” (11:2).
- **Notes:** Leviticus is almost all law. It is held in two flavors whose lessons frame each run of laws and teach its few events (Aaron’s ordination, Nadab and Abihu, the blasphemer); no lesson is built on a law list alone.

## Numbers

### 30. Israel: Counted and Camped at Sinai
- **Range:** Numbers 1:1–10:10
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Numbers 1–2 — Every Man Counted
     - God says, “Take ye the sum of all the congregation”; every man of war is counted by tribe, and the tribes camp around the tabernacle, each “by his own standard” (1:2–3, 1:46, 2:2).
  2. Numbers 3–4 — The Levites Set Apart
     - The Levites are taken instead of the firstborn, for “the Levites shall be mine”, and each family is given its work carrying the tabernacle (3:12, 3:45, 4:15).
  3. Numbers 5–6 — The LORD Bless Thee
     - Laws for a clean camp and the Nazarite vow, then the blessing Aaron is to speak: “The LORD bless thee, and keep thee” and “The LORD make his face shine upon thee” (5:2, 6:2, 6:24–25).
  4. Numbers 7–8 — Gifts from the Princes
     - When the tabernacle is set up, the twelve princes bring their offerings, Moses hears the voice speaking “from off the mercy seat”, and the Levites are cleansed for service (7:1–2, 7:89, 8:6).
  5. Numbers 9:1–10:10 — The Cloud That Led the Camp

### 42. Moses: Justice Among Neighbours
- **Range:** Deuteronomy 19–26
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Deuteronomy 19:1–21:9 — No Innocent Blood in the Land

  2. Deuteronomy 21:10–23:14 — Holy in Home and Camp

### 43. Moses: Choose Life
- **Range:** Deuteronomy 27–30
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Deuteronomy 27 — Twelve Curses and Twelve Amens
     - Over Jordan the law is written on plastered stones, for “this day thou art become the people of the LORD”; six tribes stand on Gerizim and six on Ebal, and after each curse “all the people shall say, Amen” (27:2–3, 27:9, 27:12–13, 27:15).
  2. Deuteronomy 28:1–14 — The Head and Not the Tail
     - If Israel hearkens, “Blessed shalt thou be in the city, and blessed shalt thou be in the field”, and the LORD will make them “the head, and not the tail” (28:1, 28:3, 28:13).
  3. Deuteronomy 28:15–68 — Scattered Among All People
     - If they will not hearken, curses will overtake them; a nation from far will besiege them, and the LORD will “scatter thee among all people” (28:15, 28:49, 28:52, 28:64).
  4. Deuteronomy 29 — A Covenant Made in Moab
     - Beside the covenant at Horeb, Israel enters a covenant in Moab; for forty years “your clothes are not waxen old upon you”, and “The secret things belong unto the LORD our God” (29:1, 29:5, 29:12, 29:29).
  5. Deuteronomy 30 — The Word Is Very Near
