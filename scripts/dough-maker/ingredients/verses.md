How to read an entry:
- **Title** — after the book name, what the player learns from the whole flavor, as this recipe teaches and tests it (2.10).
- **Range** — the verses the flavor teaches and reveals (Question Rules 2.12 format).
- **Parallel** — other accounts of the same events: revealed after the base, never a question source (2.17).
- **Frame** — verses outside the range that name who speaks and to whom, for lessons whose own verses do not (7.V9); each lesson note says which Frame verse it uses.
- **Lessons** — one numbered line per lesson: its range, then its title in plain words (2.10). The line under it says what the lesson teaches toward the flavor title.
- **Split option** — where the flavor could become two flavors that each still have 2–5 lessons (2.4), or "None".
- **People / Places** — who and where the reference layer links to the range, most-mentioned first.
- **Turn** — the words in the text where the section begins.

### 1. Verses: God So Loved the World
- **Range:** John 3:14–17; Romans 5:6–9; 1 John 4:4–9; Zephaniah 3:14–18; Jeremiah 31:1–4
- **Frame:** John 3:9–10; Romans 1:1; Romans 1:7; Zephaniah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. John 3:14–17 — John 3:16
     - Frame John 3:9–10 shows Nicodemus asking “How can these things be?” and “Jesus answered and said unto him”. After saying “so must the Son of man be lifted up”, Jesus says “God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son”, so that believers have everlasting life (3:14, 3:16).
  2. Romans 5:6–9 — Romans 5:8
     - Paul writes to the saints in Rome (Frame Romans 1:1 names Paul as the writer; Romans 1:7 names the readers in Rome): “God commendeth his love toward us”, for Christ died for us “while we were yet sinners” (5:6, 5:8).
  3. 1 John 4:4–9 — 1 John 4:8
     - Writing to “little children”, the letter says, “greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world”; then, “He that loveth not knoweth not God; for God is love” (4:4, 4:8). Scene questions do not test 1 John 4:7, another lesson’s verse.

     - The prophet calls the “daughter of Zion” to sing, for “the LORD, is in the midst of thee”; he says, “he will rest in his love, he will joy over thee with singing” (3:14–15, 3:17). Frame Zephaniah 1:1 names Zephaniah as the prophet.

     - “saith the LORD” to “all the families of Israel”: “I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee” (31:1, 31:3).
