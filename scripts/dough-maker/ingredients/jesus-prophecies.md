How to read an entry:
- **Title** — after the book name, what the player learns from the whole flavor, as this recipe teaches and tests it (2.10).
- **Range** — the verses the flavor teaches and reveals (Question Rules 2.12 format).
- **Parallel** — other accounts of the same events: revealed after the base, never a question source (2.17).
- **Frame** — verses outside the range that lesson 1 also draws on (Letters: who wrote it, to whom, and why, 7.L2).
- **Lessons** — one numbered line per lesson: its range, then its title in plain words (2.10). The line under it says what the lesson teaches toward the flavor title.
- **The Fulfillment** — lesson 2's range is the NT passages; when lessons are written, lesson 2 also reveals the prophecy again, labeled Prophecy.
- **Split option** — where the flavor could become two flavors that each still have 2–5 lessons (2.4), or "None".
- **People / Places** — who and where the reference layer links to the range, most-mentioned first.
- **Turn** — the words in the text where the section begins.

## Jesus Prophecies

### 1. Micah: Born in Bethlehem
- **Range:** Micah 5:2–5; Matthew 2:1–6
- **Frame:** Micah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Micah 5:2–5 — The Promise
     - Micah says to Bethlehem Ephratah, “out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel”, one who “shall stand and feed in the strength of the LORD” (Micah 5:2, 5:4).
  2. Matthew 2:1–6 — The Fulfillment
     - Wise men ask where the King of the Jews is born, and the chief priests answer, “In Bethlehem of Judaea: for thus it is written by the prophet” (Matthew 2:1–2, 2:4–6).
- **Split option:** None. Each prophecy is exactly 2 lessons (2.4).
- **People (5):** Herod, Jesus Christ, Ephratah, God, Jacob (Israel)
- **Places (4):** Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Judea, Bethlehem Ephrathah
- **Turn:** “But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah” (Micah 5:2).
- **Notes:** List row 4. Blue (not disputed); NT link: Quoted. Matthew 2:5–6 quotes Micah 5:2.

### 2. Isaiah: Sent to Free the Captives
- **Range:** Isaiah 61:1–2; Luke 4:16–21; Acts 10:38
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Isaiah 61:1–2 — The Promise
     - The speaker says “the LORD hath anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek”, “to proclaim liberty to the captives”, and “To proclaim the acceptable year of the LORD” (Isaiah 61:1–2).
  2. Luke 4:16–21; Acts 10:38 — The Fulfillment
     - In the synagogue at Nazareth Jesus reads from Esaias, closes the book after “To preach the acceptable year of the Lord”, and says, “This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears” (Luke 4:16–21).
- **Split option:** None. Each prophecy is exactly 2 lessons (2.4).
- **People (4):** God, Holy Spirit, Jesus Christ, Isaiah
- **Places (1):** Nazareth
- **Turn:** “to proclaim liberty to the captives” (Isaiah 61:1).
- **Notes:** List row 46. Blue (not disputed); NT link: Quoted. Luke 4:18–21 quotes it. The list gives Isaiah 61:1–2a because Jesus stops before "the day of vengeance"; a reveal shows whole verses, so lesson 1 holds all of 61:2; the lesson tests only up to “the acceptable year of the LORD”.

### 3. Deuteronomy: A Prophet Like Moses
- **Range:** Deuteronomy 18:15; Deuteronomy 18:18–19; Matthew 21:11; Luke 7:16; Luke 24:19; John 6:14; John 7:40; Acts 3:18–22
- **Frame:** Deuteronomy 5:1; Deuteronomy 18:16–17
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Deuteronomy 18:15; Deuteronomy 18:18–19 — The Promise
     - Moses tells Israel that the LORD “will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee”, and tells how at Horeb “the LORD said unto me” that He “will put my words in his mouth” (Deuteronomy 18:15–18).
  2. Matthew 21:11; Luke 7:16; Luke 24:19; John 6:14; John 7:40; Acts 3:18–22 — The Fulfillment
     - Crowds call Jesus “Jesus the prophet of Nazareth” and “that prophet that should come into the world”; in Acts the hearers are told “Moses truly said unto the fathers, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up” (Matthew 21:11; John 6:14; Acts 3:22).
- **Split option:** None. Each prophecy is exactly 2 lessons (2.4).
- **People (3):** Jesus Christ, God, Moses
- **Places (2):** Nazareth, Galilee
- **Turn:** “a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me” (Deuteronomy 18:15).
- **Notes:** List row 27. Blue (not disputed); NT link: Quoted. Acts 3:22–23 quotes Deuteronomy 18:15, 19; the list's range stops at Acts 3:22, so 3:23 is not included. Deuteronomy 18:16–17 (the people's request at Horeb and “the LORD said unto me”) is a Frame line, not part of the pair: it names who speaks verse 18.

### 4. Psalms: Seated at God’s Right Hand
- **Range:** Psalm 110:1; Matthew 22:41–45; Mark 12:35–37; Mark 16:19; Acts 7:56; Romans 1:3; Ephesians 1:20; Hebrews 1:3
- **Frame:** Mark 12:36
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Psalm 110:1 — The Promise
     - “David himself said by the Holy Ghost” what the LORD said to David’s Lord: “The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool” (Psalm 110:1; Mark 12:36).
  2. Matthew 22:41–45; Mark 12:35–37; Mark 16:19; Acts 7:56; Romans 1:3; Ephesians 1:20; Hebrews 1:3 — The Fulfillment
     - Jesus asks the Pharisees of Christ, “whose son is he?”, then “How then doth David in spirit call him Lord”, quoting the psalm; Mark says He “sat on the right hand of God” (Matthew 22:42–44; Mark 12:36; Mark 16:19).
- **Split option:** None. Each prophecy is exactly 2 lessons (2.4).
- **People (4):** Jesus Christ, God, David, Holy Spirit
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** “The LORD said unto my Lord” (Psalm 110:1).
- **Notes:** List row 102. Blue (not disputed); NT link: Quoted. Matthew 22:44 and Mark 12:36 quote it. Staging has no heading for Psalm 110, so lesson 1 shows Mark 12:36 from the pair’s own NT side to name the speaker: David, giving the words the LORD said to “my Lord”.

### 5. Jeremiah: A New Covenant
- **Range:** Jeremiah 31:31–34; Jeremiah 32:37–40; Jeremiah 50:5; Mark 14:22–24; Luke 22:20; Hebrews 8:8–12; Hebrews 10:15–17
- **Frame:** Jeremiah 1:1–2
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Jeremiah 31:31–34; Jeremiah 32:37–40; Jeremiah 50:5 — The Promise
     - The LORD says, “I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel”, “I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts”, and “I will remember their sin no more” (Jeremiah 31:31, 31:33–34, 32:40, 50:5).
  2. Mark 14:22–24; Luke 22:20; Hebrews 8:8–12; Hebrews 10:15–17 — The Fulfillment
     - As they eat, Jesus takes the cup and says, “This is my blood of the new testament”; Luke has “This cup is the new testament in my blood”, and Hebrews quotes Jeremiah: “I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel” (Mark 14:22–24; Luke 22:20; Hebrews 8:8–12, 10:16–17).
- **Split option:** None. Each prophecy is exactly 2 lessons (2.4).
- **People (3):** God, Jesus Christ, Holy Spirit
- **Places (2):** Egypt, Zion
- **Turn:** “I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel” (Jeremiah 31:31).
- **Notes:** List row 26. Blue (not disputed); NT link: Quoted. Hebrews 8:8–12 and 10:16–17 quote Jeremiah 31:31–34; Jeremiah 32:37–40 and 50:5 are not quoted. Lesson 2 is trimmed from the list's six passages to Mark 14:22–24 (the only one that names Jesus), Luke 22:20, and the two Hebrews quotes.

### 6. Zechariah: The Sheep Will Be Scattered
- **Range:** Zechariah 13:7; Matthew 26:31; Matthew 26:56; Mark 14:27; John 16:32
- **Frame:** Zechariah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Zechariah 13:7 — The Promise
     - The LORD of hosts says, “Awake, O sword, against my shepherd”, “smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered” (Zechariah 13:7).
  2. Matthew 26:31; Matthew 26:56; Mark 14:27; John 16:32 — The Fulfillment
     - Jesus tells the disciples, “All ye shall be offended because of me this night: for it is written, I will smite the shepherd”, and later “all the disciples forsook him, and fled” (Matthew 26:31, 26:56; Mark 14:27).
- **Split option:** None. Each prophecy is exactly 2 lessons (2.4).
- **People (2):** God, Jesus Christ
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** “smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered” (Zechariah 13:7).
- **Notes:** List row 91. Blue (not disputed); NT link: Quoted. Matthew 26:31 and Mark 14:27 quote it. Applied the list's note ("consider using 13:7 only"): Zechariah 13:6 dropped.

### 7. Isaiah: A Lamb to the Slaughter
- **Range:** Isaiah 53:7; Isaiah 53:9; Acts 8:30–35; 1 Peter 2:22
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Isaiah 53:7; Isaiah 53:9 — The Promise
     - Isaiah says, “he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth”, and “neither was any deceit in his mouth” (Isaiah 53:7, 53:9).
  2. Acts 8:30–35; 1 Peter 2:22 — The Fulfillment
     - In Acts a man reading Esaias asks Philip, “of whom speaketh the prophet this?”, and Philip “preached unto him Jesus”; 1 Peter says “Who did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth” (Acts 8:32, 8:34–35; 1 Peter 2:22).
- **Split option:** None. Each prophecy is exactly 2 lessons (2.4).
- **People (3):** Phillip the Evangelist, Isaiah, Jesus Christ
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** “he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter” (Isaiah 53:7).
- **Notes:** List rows 64, 85, and 86, merged (one prophecy). Orange (disputed); NT link: Quoted. Dispute: Isaiah 52:13–53:12: Messiah vs Israel (Corporate) vs Typological. Acts 8:32–33 quotes Isaiah 53:7–8; 1 Peter 2:22 quotes Isaiah 53:9. Back to the list's verses: Isaiah 53:8 and 1 Peter 2:21, 2:23 had been added and are out. The other listed verses (the silent-before-accusers passages, John 1:29, 1:36, 1 Peter 1:19, Revelation 5:6) do not quote it and were left out.
