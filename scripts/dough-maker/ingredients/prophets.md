How to read an entry:
- **Title** — after the book name, what the player learns from the whole flavor, as this recipe teaches and tests it (2.10).
- **Range** — the verses the flavor teaches and reveals (Question Rules 2.12 format).
- **Parallel** — other accounts of the same events: revealed after the base, never a question source (2.17).
- **Frame** — verses outside the range that lesson 1 also draws on (Letters: who wrote it, to whom, and why, 7.L2).
- **Lessons** — one numbered line per lesson: its range, then its title in plain words (2.10). The line under it says what the lesson teaches toward the flavor title.
- **Split option** — where the flavor could become two flavors that each still have 2–5 lessons (2.4), or "None".
- **People / Places** — who and where the reference layer links to the range, most-mentioned first.
- **Turn** — the words in the text where the section begins.

## Isaiah

### 1. Isaiah: A Rebel Nation Called Back
- **Range:** Isaiah 1
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Isaiah 1:1–9 — Children Who Rebelled
     - Isaiah’s vision for Judah and Jerusalem in the days of four kings opens with God’s charge: He raised children and “they have rebelled against me”, so their land lies desolate (1:1–2, 1:4, 1:7).
  2. Isaiah 1:10–20 — Sins Made White as Snow
     - God tells Jerusalem’s rulers He is weary of their offerings because their hands are full of blood, and calls them to turn: “though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow” (1:11, 1:15–18).
  3. Isaiah 1:21–31 — The Faithful City Restored
     - The faithful city has turned to murder and bribes, so God will purge it like metal and give it back its judges: “The city of righteousness, the faithful city” (1:21, 1:23, 1:25–26).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (7):** God, Ahaz, Amoz, Hezekiah, Isaiah, Jotham, Uzziah
- **Places (4):** Gomorrah, Sodom, Zion, Jerusalem
- **Turn:** "The vision of Isaiah the son of Amoz, which he saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem" (1:1).

### 2. Isaiah: Only the LORD Will Be Lifted Up
- **Range:** Isaiah 2–4
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Isaiah 2 — The Day the Proud Are Humbled
     - A new word for Judah and Jerusalem: all nations will come to learn God’s ways, but Judah is full of idols, and on that day “the LORD alone shall be exalted” (2:1–3, 2:8, 2:11).
  2. Isaiah 3:1–15 — Crushing the Poor
     - Why Jerusalem will fall: its words and deeds are against the LORD, and its leaders “grind the faces of the poor”, so God takes away its supplies and rulers (3:1–2, 3:8, 3:14–15).
  3. Isaiah 3:16–4:6 — Zion Washed Clean
     - The proud daughters of Zion lose their finery, but God will wash away Zion’s filth, and all left there “shall be called holy”, sheltered by a cloud by day and fire by night (3:16, 3:24, 4:3–5).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (4):** God, Jacob (Israel), Amoz, Isaiah
- **Places (7):** Jerusalem, Zion, Bashan, Lebanon, Mount Zion, Sodom, Tarshish
- **Turn:** "The word that Isaiah the son of Amoz saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem" (2:1).

### 3. Isaiah: God’s Vineyard Gives Wild Grapes
- **Range:** Isaiah 5
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Isaiah 5:1–7 — The Song of the Vineyard
     - Isaiah sings of a vineyard given every care that brought forth wild grapes, and the song says what it is: “the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel” (5:1–2, 5:4, 5:7).
  2. Isaiah 5:8–30 — Woe to Those Who Call Evil Good
     - The wild grapes named: greed, drunkenness, and those who “call evil good, and good evil”; because they cast away God’s law, a nation from far will come against them (5:8, 5:11, 5:20, 5:24, 5:26).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (1):** God
- **Places (1):** Jerusalem
- **Turn:** "Now will I sing to my wellbeloved a song of my beloved touching his vineyard" (5:1).
- **Notes:** The vineyard is the one picture here the text explains (5:7), so the lesson may say what it stands for (7.P5).

### 4. Isaiah: The Holy LORD Sends Isaiah
- **Range:** Isaiah 6
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Isaiah 6:1–7 — Isaiah Sees the LORD’s Throne
     - In the year king Uzziah died, Isaiah sees the LORD on His throne and hears “Holy, holy, holy”; he cries that he is unclean, and a coal from the altar takes his sin away (6:1, 6:3, 6:5–7).
  2. Isaiah 6:8–13 — Isaiah Offers to Go
     - God asks “Whom shall I send?” and Isaiah answers; his message is to a people who will hear but not understand, until the land is laid waste, with a holy seed left (6:8–9, 6:11, 6:13).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (2):** God, Uzziah
- **Places (0):** —
- **Turn:** "In the year that king Uzziah died I saw also the Lord sitting upon a throne" (6:1).

### 5. Isaiah: God Is with Us
- **Range:** Isaiah 7:1–9:7
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Isaiah 7:1–9 — Ahaz Told Not to Fear
     - When Syria and Israel come against Jerusalem, God sends Isaiah to king Ahaz: “fear not… It shall not stand”, and “If ye will not believe, surely ye shall not be established” (7:1, 7:4, 7:7, 7:9).
  2. Isaiah 7:10–25 — The Sign of Immanuel
     - Ahaz will not ask for a sign, so the Lord gives one, a son called Immanuel; before the child is grown, both kings will be gone, and the king of Assyria will come on Judah (7:12, 7:14, 7:16–17).
  3. Isaiah 8:1–10 — A Flood from Assyria
     - Isaiah’s son is named, and before the child can cry “My father, and my mother”, Assyria will take the riches of Damascus and the spoil of Samaria; because this people refused “the waters of Shiloah that go softly”, Assyria will flood Judah, yet the nations’ plans fail, “for God is with us” (8:3–4, 8:6–8, 8:10).
  4. Isaiah 8:11–9:7 — A Child Is Born to Us
     - God tells Isaiah to fear the LORD, not what the people fear, and to hold to the law; after darkness, a great light, and a child whose name is “The Prince of Peace” (8:12–13, 8:20, 9:2, 9:6).
- **Split option:** At 8:1 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (20):** God, Ephraim, Remaliah, Ahaz, Rezin, David, Maher-shalal-hash-baz, Isaiah, Jacob (Israel), Jeberechiah, Jotham, Midian, Naphtali, Pekah, Shear-jashub, Tabeal, Urijah, Uzziah, Zebulun, Zechariah
- **Places (16):** Assyria, Syria, Damascus, Jerusalem, River, Samaria, Egypt, Field, Galilee, Jordan, Kingdom of Judah, Midian, Mount Zion, Naphtali, Shiloah, Zebulun
- **Turn:** "And it came to pass in the days of Ahaz the son of Jotham… king of Judah" (7:1).
- **Notes:** One thread runs through the flavor: the child Immanuel (7:14, 8:8) and the words “God is with us” (8:10). Isaiah never says what the name means, so the lessons do not teach a meaning for it.

### 6. Isaiah: Assyria, the Rod of God’s Anger
- **Range:** Isaiah 9:8–10:34
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Isaiah 9:8–10:4 — Too Proud to Turn to God
     - A word against proud Israel: though struck again and again, “the people turneth not unto him that smiteth them”, and four times comes “his hand is stretched out still” (9:9–10, 9:12–13, 10:4).
  2. Isaiah 10:5–19 — Assyria Boasts Against God
     - God sends Assyria as “the rod of mine anger” against His people, but Assyria boasts it did all by its own strength, so God will punish it: “Shall the axe boast itself against him that heweth therewith?” (10:5–6, 10:13, 10:15).
  3. Isaiah 10:20–34 — The Few Left Will Return
     - In that day a remnant will lean on the LORD, and Zion is told “be not afraid of the Assyrian”, for the LORD will cut the proud army down as it marches on Jerusalem (10:20–21, 10:24, 10:32–33).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (7):** God, Jacob (Israel), Ephraim, Manasseh (son of Joseph), Midian, Oreb, Rezin
- **Places (25):** Jerusalem, Samaria, Assyria, Zion, Aiath, Anathoth, Arpad, Calno, Carchemish, Damascus, Egypt, Gallim, Geba, Gebim, Gibeah, Hamath, Laishah, Lebanon, Madmenah, Michmash, Midian, Migron, Mount Zion, Nob, Ramah
- **Turn:** "The Lord sent a word into Jacob, and it hath lighted upon Israel" (9:8).

### 7. Isaiah: A Branch from Jesse’s Roots
- **Range:** Isaiah 11–12
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1

  2. Isaiah 11:10–12:6 — A Song for That Day
     - In that day the Lord will gather His scattered people from the four corners of the earth, and they will sing, “Behold, God is my salvation; I will trust, and not be afraid” (11:11–12, 12:1–2).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (5):** God, Jesse, Ephraim, Esau (Edom), Moab
- **Places (12):** Assyria, Egypt, Cush, Edom, Egyptian Sea, Elam, Hamath, Moab, Pathros, River, Shinar, Zion

### 8. Isaiah: Babylon Will Be Overthrown
- **Range:** Isaiah 13:1–14:27
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Isaiah 13 — The Day of the LORD on Babylon
     - Isaiah’s message against Babylon: the LORD musters an army, will stir up the Medes against it, and proud Babylon will be “as when God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah” (13:1, 13:4, 13:11, 13:17, 13:19).
  2. Isaiah 14:1–27 — Mocking the King of Babylon
     - When God has mercy on Israel, they will mock the king of Babylon, who said “I will be like the most High” yet is brought down; and the LORD swears to break the Assyrian too (14:1, 14:4, 14:14–15, 14:24–25).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (5):** God, Amoz, Isaiah, Jacob (Israel), Lucifer
- **Places (5):** Babylon, Gomorrah, Lebanon, Ophir, Sodom

### 9. Isaiah: Judah’s Neighbors Will Fall
- **Range:** Isaiah 14:28–17:14
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 2
  1. Isaiah 14:28–16:14 — Philistia and Moab Will Howl

  2. Isaiah 17 — Damascus Becomes a Ruin
     - Damascus will be a ruinous heap and Israel’s glory made thin, because “thou hast forgotten the God of thy salvation”; in that day a man will look to his Maker, not his idols (17:1, 17:4, 17:7–8, 17:10).

- **Turn:** "In the year that king Ahaz died was this burden" (14:28).

### 10. Isaiah: What Will Happen to Egypt
- **Range:** Isaiah 18–20
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Isaiah 18 — A Gift from a Far Nation
     - A word to the land beyond the rivers of Ethiopia: the LORD waits and watches, cuts off the branches before harvest, and then that people brings a present to mount Zion (18:1, 18:4–5, 18:7).
  2. Isaiah 19:1–15 — The LORD Comes into Egypt
     - The LORD rides on a cloud into Egypt: its idols tremble, Egyptian fights Egyptian, the river is dried up, and Pharaoh’s wise counsellors become fools (19:1–2, 19:5, 19:11).
  3. Isaiah 19:16–25 — Egypt Will Know the LORD
     - In that day Egypt will fear, then cry to the LORD, and He will heal them; there will be an altar to the LORD in Egypt, and Israel will be a blessing with Egypt and Assyria (19:16, 19:19–22, 19:24–25).
  4. Isaiah 20 — Isaiah Walks Barefoot
     - When Assyria takes Ashdod, Isaiah walks naked and barefoot three years as a sign: Assyria will lead Egypt and Ethiopia away captive, so those who trusted them will be ashamed (20:1–5).
- **Split option:** At 19:1 (1 + 3 lessons) would leave 1 lesson; none possible. None.
- **People (7):** God, Isaiah, Amoz, Canaan, Pharaoh, Sennacherib, Tartan
- **Places (10):** Egypt, Assyria, Cush, Zoan, Ashdod, Canaan, Destruction, Memphis, Mount Zion, Nile
- **Turn:** "Woe to the land shadowing with wings, which is beyond the rivers of Ethiopia" (18:1).

### 11. Isaiah: Proud Cities Brought Low
- **Range:** Isaiah 21–23
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Isaiah 21 — The Watchman Sees Babylon Fall
     - A watchman sees chariots and cries “Babylon is fallen, is fallen”; then two short messages: Seir asks “Watchman, what of the night?”, and within a year the glory of Kedar in Arabia will fail (21:6–9, 21:11–12, 21:16).
  2. Isaiah 22:1–14 — Feasting Instead of Weeping
     - A message to Jerusalem, “the valley of vision”: they mend their walls but “have not looked unto the maker thereof”, and when God calls to weeping they say “let us eat and drink; for to morrow we shall die” (22:1, 22:9–13).
  3. Isaiah 22:15–25 — Shebna Replaced by Eliakim
     - Isaiah is sent to Shebna, the treasurer cutting himself a fine tomb: he will be tossed into a far country, and God will give his office and the key of the house of David to Eliakim (22:15–18, 22:20–22).
  4. Isaiah 23 — The Ships of Tarshish Howl
     - The ships of Tarshish howl because Tyre, the rich city of merchants, is laid waste: “The LORD of hosts hath purposed it, to stain the pride of all glory” (23:1, 23:8–9, 23:14).
- **Split option:** At 22:15 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (6):** God, David, Kedar, Eliakim, Hilkiah, Shebna
- **Places (21):** Tyre, Tarshish, Sidon, Cyprus, Elam, Jerusalem, Kedar, Nile, Arabia, Assyria, Babylon, Canaan, Dumah, Egypt, Forest, Kir, Media, Negeb, Seir, Shihor, Tema
- **Turn:** "The burden of the desert of the sea" (21:1).
- **Notes:** Lesson 1 holds three whole messages (21:1, 21:11, 21:13); the last two are too short to be lessons alone. Isaiah 23 was split in two halves; it is one message and is now one lesson.

### 12. Isaiah: The LORD Will Reign in Zion
- **Range:** Isaiah 24–27
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Isaiah 24 — The LORD Empties the Earth
     - The LORD lays the whole earth waste because its people “have transgressed the laws… broken the everlasting covenant”, until “the LORD of hosts shall reign in mount Zion” (24:1, 24:5, 24:23).
  2. Isaiah 25 — Death Swallowed Up in Victory
     - A song of praise: God has been a refuge to the poor, will make a feast for all people on His mountain, and “will swallow up death in victory” and wipe away tears (25:1, 25:4, 25:6, 25:8–9).
  3. Isaiah 26 — Kept in Perfect Peace
     - A song for the land of Judah: God keeps in perfect peace the one whose mind is stayed on Him, so “Trust ye in the LORD for ever”, and His dead will live (26:1, 26:3–4, 26:19).
  4. Isaiah 27 — Israel Will Fill the World
     - In that day God guards His vineyard, Israel will “blossom and bud, and fill the face of the world with fruit”, and a great trumpet calls the lost home to worship at Jerusalem (27:2–3, 27:6, 27:13).
- **Split option:** At 26:1 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (3):** God, Jacob (Israel), Moab
- **Places (7):** Jerusalem, Assyria, Brook of Egypt, Egypt, Euphrates, Moab, Mount Zion
- **Turn:** "Behold, the LORD maketh the earth empty, and maketh it waste" (24:1).

### 13. Isaiah: A Rest They Refused
- **Range:** Isaiah 28–29
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Isaiah 28:1–13 — The Drunkards of Ephraim
     - Woe to the proud drunkards of Ephraim, and to Judah’s priest and prophet who also err through drink: God offered them rest, “yet they would not hear” (28:1, 28:7, 28:12).
  2. Isaiah 28:14–29 — A Sure Foundation in Zion
     - Jerusalem’s scornful rulers think they have a covenant with death, but God lays in Zion “a tried stone, a precious corner stone”, and “he that believeth shall not make haste” (28:14–16, 28:18).
  3. Isaiah 29:1–14 — Near with Lips, Far in Heart
     - Woe to Ariel, the city where David lived: God will bring it low, because “this people draw near me with their mouth… but have removed their heart far from me” (29:1–4, 29:13).
  4. Isaiah 29:15–24 — The Deaf Will Hear the Book
     - Woe to those who hide their plans from God, as if clay could say to the potter “He made me not”; yet in that day the deaf will hear and the meek rejoice in the Holy One (29:15–16, 29:18–19).
- **Split option:** At 29:1 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (5):** God, Ephraim, Jacob (Israel), Abraham, David
- **Places (7):** Ariel, Jerusalem, Lebanon, Mount Perazim, Mount Zion, Valley of Gibeon, Zion
- **Turn:** "Woe to the crown of pride, to the drunkards of Ephraim" (28:1).

### 14. Isaiah: Strength in Quiet Trust
- **Range:** Isaiah 30–31
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Isaiah 30:1–17 — Egypt Will Help in Vain
     - Woe to Judah, the rebellious children who go down to Egypt for help without asking God; He tells them “In returning and rest shall ye be saved; in quietness and in confidence shall be your strength: and ye would not” (30:1–2, 30:7, 30:15).
  2. Isaiah 30:18–33 — The LORD Waits to Be Gracious
     - Yet the LORD waits to be gracious: Zion will weep no more, a voice will say “This is the way, walk ye in it”, and His voice will beat down the Assyrian (30:18–19, 30:21, 30:31).
  3. Isaiah 31 — The LORD Will Defend Jerusalem
     - Woe to those who trust Egypt’s horses, for “the Egyptians are men, and not God”; like birds flying, the LORD will defend Jerusalem, so “Turn ye unto him” (31:1, 31:3, 31:5–6).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (3):** God, Pharaoh, Jacob (Israel)
- **Places (7):** Egypt, Jerusalem, Zion, Hanes, Mount Zion, Negeb, Zoan
- **Turn:** "Woe to the rebellious children, saith the LORD, that take counsel, but not of me" (30:1).

### 15. Isaiah: A King Who Rules with Justice
- **Range:** Isaiah 32–33
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 4
  1. Isaiah 32:1–8 — Eyes That See, Ears That Hear
     - “Behold, a king shall reign in righteousness”: under him people find shelter, eyes see and ears hear, and the wicked are no longer called noble (32:1–3, 32:5).
  2. Isaiah 32:9–20 — Careless Women Told to Tremble
     - The women at ease are told to tremble, for the land will grow thorns until the spirit is poured from on high; then “the work of righteousness shall be peace” (32:9, 32:11, 32:13, 32:15, 32:17).
  3. Isaiah 33:1–12 — Woe to the Destroyer
     - Woe to the destroyer; God’s people pray “O LORD, be gracious unto us; we have waited for thee”, and the LORD says “Now will I rise” (33:1–2, 33:10).
  4. Isaiah 33:13–24 — Who Can Dwell with God?
     - Sinners in Zion ask who can dwell with God; the one who walks righteously, and they will “see the king in his beauty”, for “the LORD is our king; he will save us” (33:14–15, 33:17, 33:22).
- **Split option:** At 33:1 (2 + 2 lessons).
- **People (1):** God
- **Places (6):** Zion, Bashan, Carmel, Jerusalem, Lebanon, Saron
- **Turn:** "Behold, a king shall reign in righteousness, and princes shall rule in judgment" (32:1).

### 17. Isaiah: Hezekiah’s Day of Trouble
- **Range:** Isaiah 36–39
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 5
  1. Isaiah 36 — Rabshakeh Mocks Judah’s Trust
     - In Hezekiah’s fourteenth year, Assyria’s officer Rabshakeh stands at Jerusalem and mocks the people: “Neither let Hezekiah make you trust in the LORD” (36:1–2, 36:4, 36:15).
  2. Isaiah 37:1–20 — Hezekiah Prays to the LORD
     - Hezekiah sends to Isaiah, who answers “Be not afraid”; when a threatening letter comes, Hezekiah spreads it before the LORD and prays “save us… that all the kingdoms of the earth may know” (37:1–2, 37:6, 37:14, 37:20).
  3. Isaiah 37:21–38 — The Angel Strikes Assyria’s Camp
     - Because Hezekiah prayed, God sends word through Isaiah that the king of Assyria “shall not come into this city”, and the angel of the LORD strikes the Assyrian camp (37:21, 37:33–36).
  4. Isaiah 38 — Fifteen More Years
     - Hezekiah is sick unto death and prays; God tells Isaiah “I have heard thy prayer… I will add unto thy days fifteen years”, with the sign of the shadow going back (38:1–2, 38:5, 38:8).
  5. Isaiah 39 — Treasures Shown to Babylon
     - Hezekiah shows Babylon’s messengers all his treasures, and Isaiah brings the word that all of it will one day be carried to Babylon (39:1–2, 39:5–7).
- **Split option:** At 38:1 (3 + 2 lessons).
- **People (22):** God, Hezekiah, Isaiah, Rabshakeh, Eliakim, Sennacherib, Shebna, Amoz, Joah, David, Adrammelech, Ahaz, Asaph, Asaph (Levite), Baladan, Esar-haddon, Hilkiah, Hilkiah, Merodach-baladan, Pharaoh, Sharezer, Tirhakah
- **Places (23):** Assyria, Jerusalem, Babylon, Arpad, Egypt, Hamath, Lachish, Sepharvaim, Cush, Eden, Field, Gozan, Haran, Hena, Ivvah, Lebanon, Libnah, Mount Zion, Nineveh, Rezeph, Samaria, Telassar, Zion

- **Notes:** these chapters are mostly events, with God’s messages inside them (37:6–7, 37:21–35, 38:5–8, 39:5–7). They are kept here because Isaiah delivers each message; they are also a Stories passage.

### 18. Isaiah: Here Is Your God
- **Range:** Isaiah 40–41
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Isaiah 40:1–11 — Make a Highway for Our God
     - A new word of comfort for Jerusalem: her iniquity is pardoned, a voice cries “Prepare ye the way of the LORD”, and “the word of our God shall stand for ever”; Judah’s cities are told “Behold your God!” (40:1–3, 40:8–9).
  2. Isaiah 40:12–31 — The Everlasting God Never Tires
     - No one can be compared to God, who measured the waters and calls the stars by name; Israel thinks God has forgotten them, but “they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength” (40:12, 40:18, 40:26–28, 40:31).
  3. Isaiah 41 — God Will Hold Israel’s Hand
     - God calls the nations to judgment and tells Israel, His chosen servant, “Fear thou not; for I am with thee”, while the idols are challenged to tell the future and cannot (41:1, 41:8–10, 41:13, 41:22–24).
- **Split option:** None. Any cut would leave a flavor with 1 lesson (2.4).
- **People (4):** God, Jacob (Israel), Abraham, Holy Spirit
- **Places (3):** Jerusalem, Zion, Lebanon
- **Turn:** "Comfort ye, comfort ye my people, saith your God" (40:1).

### 19. Isaiah: God’s Chosen Servant
- **Range:** Isaiah 42
- **Frame:** Isaiah 1:1
- **Lessons:** 3
  1. Isaiah 42:1–9 — A Servant God Delights In
