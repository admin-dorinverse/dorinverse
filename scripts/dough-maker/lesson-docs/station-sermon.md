# Station: Sermon

This file covers the content for the Sermon station.

## What it is

A skeptic, the Heckler, pushes back on what the passage teaches. The baker answers each objection with what the speaker actually said. It's used for sermons, speeches, letters and prophets' messages. When the passage has more than one voice, like God's and the people's in Isaiah 53, each right answer says who is speaking.

## The passage text

- The passage text is provided in the **BSB** (Berean Standard Bible).
- Every true fact and every verse number comes from the BSB.

## The Heckler

- A skeptic voice that pushes back. Never mocking or crude.
- **Written for adults.** No scenarios about school, parents or anything aimed at kids. Kids can still play.

## The objections

- **5 to 7 objections** per passage.
- **Every objection is one the passage answers.** The Heckler can raise doubts, but the answer always comes from the text.
- When the speaker raises an objection himself, the Heckler can use it, like Paul does in Romans 6:1.

## The answers

- **Every right answer is the speaker's own point.** It retells the speaker's words closely, in BSB terms.
- **Don't let the right answer stand out.** All three answers sound alike. If one sounds like Bible wording and the others sound modern, rewrite them so they match. The verse shows after the pick.

## Answer rules

- Each objection has **3 answers** and **one try**.
- All three answers are the same kind of thing, about the same length and the same tone. None are silly.
- Each wrong answer must be false for that moment.
- The code shuffles where the right answer sits. The agent doesn't need to.

## Output for each objection

- The Heckler's objection
- The 3 answers
- The right answer
- The verse that backs it

## Cookies

- Every right answer adds **1 cherry cookie** to the box.
- A wrong answer adds nothing.

## Example: Romans 6:1–14

1. **Heckler:** "If grace covers every sin, why not keep sinning? More sin, more grace."
   - A. As long as you feel sorry afterward, sinning is fine
   - **B. No, we died to sin, so we can't go on living in it** *(6:1–2)*
   - C. Grace only covers small sins, so the big ones still count
2. **Heckler:** "Died to sin? When did that happen?"
   - **A. When we were baptized into Christ, we were baptized into His death** *(6:3)*
   - B. When we grew old enough to know right from wrong
   - C. Slowly, a little more each time we try harder
3. **Heckler:** "My old self still shows up every day."
   - A. Ignore it long enough and eventually it will leave you
   - **B. Our old self was crucified with Him, so we're not slaves to sin** *(6:6)*
   - C. That old self is who you really are underneath it all
4. **Heckler:** "Why would dying set anybody free?"
   - **A. Because anyone who has died has been freed from sin** *(6:7)*
   - B. Because after you die, nothing you did matters anymore
   - C. Because every morning you get a brand-new start
5. **Heckler:** "So what am I supposed to do now?"
   - **A. Count yourself dead to sin but alive to God in Christ Jesus** *(6:11)*
   - B. Wait until God takes away every temptation you face
   - C. Keep a list of your sins and check them off one by one
6. **Heckler:** "My body still wants what it wants."
   - A. Then let it, since only what's in your heart really counts
   - **B. Don't give your body over to sin, but give yourself to God** *(6:13)*
   - C. Stay away from people so you can't be tempted at all
7. **Heckler:** "Sin is just too strong for me."
   - A. Try harder and keep every rule you possibly can
   - B. Then stay under the law until you're strong enough
   - **C. Sin shall not be your master, because you're under grace** *(6:14)*
