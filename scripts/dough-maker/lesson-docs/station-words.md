# Station: Words

This file covers the content for the Words station.

## What it is

A single word from the passage texts the baker. At first it shows up as **Unknown**. Through its texts, the baker works out what it means, names it, and then follows it through the passage and across the Bible. Every text is a question or a sentence to finish, with 3 answers.

## The passage text

- The passage text is provided in the **BSB** (Berean Standard Bible).
- Every true fact and every verse number comes from the BSB.

## The word

- **One word per station.**
- The word must be flagged `BSB Eligible` in the database (from Easton's Bible Dictionary), and it must appear in the BSB text of the passage.
- **It carries real weight.** The baker needs it to understand the passage, and it matters across the Bible.
- If no word in the passage meets that bar, the passage gets no Words station.

## How the word talks

- It always speaks in **first person**. It's the one texting.
- It has **no personality**, and it never sounds like Papa Dorin.
- **Written for adults.** No scenarios about school, parents or anything aimed at kids. Kids can still play.

## The flow

1. **Unknown, clue 1.** The word describes what it is.
2. **Unknown, clue 2.** The word shows up in the passage, as a second clue to what it means.
3. **Save the contact.** The word gives a verse from the passage with itself blanked out, and the baker picks the name to save it under.
4. **Confirm the meaning.**
5. **Again in this passage.** *Optional.* Only when the word appears again in the passage.
6. **Elsewhere in the Bible.** The same word, in the BSB, outside the passage.
7. **Why it matters.** How the word ties into the passage's message.
8. **Use it today.** The baker finishes a modern sentence that uses the word.

**Steps 1 and 2:** the word is giving clues about itself. Every Unknown text has to help the baker work out what the word is.

## Answer rules

- Each text has **3 answers** and **one try**.
- All three answers are the same kind of thing, about the same length and the same tone. None are silly.
- Each wrong answer must be false for that moment.
- The code shuffles where the right answer sits. The agent doesn't need to.

## Output for each text

- The question
- The 3 answers
- The right answer
- The verse that backs it. A text about the word's meaning or a modern sentence can have none.

## Cookies

- Every right answer adds **1 bubblegum cookie** to the box.
- A wrong answer adds nothing.

## Example 1: sackcloth (Jonah 3)

1. **Unknown:** "I'm rough, plain cloth. Nobody wears me to look good. So when would someone put me on?"
   - A. At a wedding feast
   - **B. When they're grieving or sorry for their sin** *(3:5, 3:8)*
   - C. When they lead an army into battle
2. **Unknown:** "In Nineveh, even the king took off his royal robe and covered himself with me. What does that tell you about me?"
   - **A. I'm what people wear to humble themselves** *(3:6)*
   - B. I'm what kings wear into battle
   - C. I'm what priests wear in the temple
3. **Unknown:** "'They proclaimed a fast and dressed in ___, from the greatest of them to the least.' That's me. Figured out who's texting? Save my contact as…"
   - A. ashes
   - **B. sackcloth** *(3:5)*
   - C. linen
4. **sackcloth:** "So when someone puts me on, they're…"
   - A. Dressing up for a feast
   - **B. Showing grief and sorrow over sin**
   - C. Getting ready for a long journey
5. **sackcloth:** "The king wanted more than people wearing me. Who else did he say should be covered with me?"
   - A. Only the priests
   - **B. The animals too** *(3:8, "both man and beast")*
   - C. Only the king's nobles
6. **sackcloth:** "Jesus mentioned me. He said Tyre and Sidon would have repented long ago in sackcloth and… what?"
   - **A. ashes** *(Matthew 11:21)*
   - B. tears
   - C. oil
7. **sackcloth:** "Nineveh put me on. But what did God see when He looked at them?"
   - A. How long they had fasted
   - **B. That they had turned from their evil ways** *(3:10)*
   - C. How costly their offerings were
8. **sackcloth:** "Finish my sentence: After the scandal broke, the CEO showed up in sackcloth, so to speak. He…"
   - A. …announced record profits
   - **B. …admitted he was wrong and apologized publicly**
   - C. …blamed his board

## Example 2: grace (Romans 6)

1. **Unknown:** "You can't earn me, and you can't pay me back. All you can do is receive me. What am I?"
   - A. A wage for your work
   - **B. A gift you don't deserve**
   - C. A reward for doing right
2. **Unknown:** "Some people thought more sin would just bring more of me. Paul said, 'Certainly not!' What were they getting wrong about me?"
   - **A. They treated me as an excuse to keep sinning** *(6:1–2)*
   - B. They thought I had to be earned
   - C. They thought I would run out
3. **Unknown:** "'For sin shall not be your master, because you are not under law, but under ___.' That's me. Figured out who's texting? Save my contact as…"
   - A. death
   - B. works
   - **C. grace** *(6:14)*
4. **grace:** "So I am…"
   - **A. God's favor you can't earn**
   - B. A reward for keeping the law
   - C. A second chance you have to pay back
5. **grace:** "I show up again a few verses later. If you're under me and not under law, does sin stop mattering?"
   - **A. Certainly not!** *(6:15)*
   - B. Only the small sins
   - C. Not anymore
6. **grace:** "'For it is by grace you have been saved through faith, and this not from yourselves.' How does Paul say I come to you?"
   - A. Through your good works
   - B. Through keeping the law
   - **C. As the gift of God** *(Ephesians 2:8)*
7. **grace:** "Because you're under me, what can't sin be anymore?"
   - A. Forgiven
   - **B. Your master** *(6:14)*
   - C. Noticed
8. **grace:** "Finish my sentence: She showed him grace when she…"
   - **A. …forgave a loan he could never repay**
   - B. …charged him interest on the loan
   - C. …made him work off every cent
