# Papa Dorin's Prep: Walkthrough

This file covers the walkthrough part of Papa Dorin's Prep: the story cards and the choice cards. It does not cover Papa Dorin's voice or Burnt Cookies. Those have their own files (see the end).

## What it is

Papa Dorin takes the baker through the passage like a great storyteller reading it aloud. At key moments he pauses, and the baker works out what comes. That is how the baker "reads" the passage. The baker's pick never changes anything. The passage always plays out exactly as the text says.

This works for every kind of passage, not only stories. Every passage has a flow: the events of a story, the lines of a song, the steps of a letter's argument, the movement of a prophet's message. Papa Dorin moves the baker along that flow.

Most people do not like to read, so reading is kept to a minimum. Papa Dorin steps in only to carry the passage to the next moment worth choosing. The baker's choices matter just as much as what Papa Dorin tells.

## Goal

When the baker leaves Prep, they understand the whole passage without missing an important detail.

The goal is understanding the passage, not covering every verse. Some parts, like a stretch of rules, are told so the baker knows they are there, even though they are never asked about.

## The passage text

- The passage text is provided in the **BSB** (Berean Standard Bible).
- Every fact and every verse number comes from that text.

## Two kinds of cards

- **Story card:** Papa Dorin's turn. He tells the next piece of the passage. The setup for a choice is always on a story card.
- **Choice card:** the baker's turn. A question and its 3 answers.

## What bite-sized means

- **One beat per card.**
  - A story card carries one moment of the passage.
  - A choice card is one question and its answers.
  - A summary story card for a long stretch (see below) is one beat: the stretch itself.
  - Aim for one beat per story card. Two is the most. If a story card has more than two things happening, split it.
- **Short, but not stripped down.** A story card still has enough of Papa Dorin's voice and enough context that the moment makes sense.
- **Keep the baker choosing.** If several story cards go by without a choice, there is probably a moment in them worth asking about. Summary story cards for long stretches don't count here.
- **About 20 choice cards is the most, not a goal.** Use as many as the passage needs to be understood, and no more. A long passage gets more story cards, not more choice cards. Prep should never feel like a chore to get through.

## Everything moves the passage along

- Every story card moves the baker forward through the passage.
- Papa Dorin can add a storyteller's line to carry the moment, like "But the Lord isn't finished with him," as long as it adds no facts.
- Papa Dorin can retell what people in the passage say in his own words.
- When Papa Dorin quotes the passage word for word, it is the exact BSB wording.

## Choosing what to ask

- Ask about the moments that move the passage forward and the details the baker needs to understand it.
- Not every moment needs a choice card. Some are better told on a story card.

## Never give away an answer

No card ever gives away the answer to a choice card that comes later. Once a choice has been answered, the cards after it can build on that answer.

- The passage title doesn't count. It's set before Prep is written.
- When a line repeats, like a psalm's refrain, ask about it only the first time it appears.

## Long stretches that don't move the passage

Some parts of a passage don't move it forward. They hold a lot of detail in one place. For example:

- rules for everyday life
- genealogies and name lists
- census counts and numbers
- building plans, like the tabernacle or the temple
- rules for offerings and feasts
- land boundaries and town lists
- repeated reports, like each tribe bringing the same gift

The list isn't complete. If a stretch is mostly detail and not much happens, it counts.

**Not a long stretch:** speeches, teaching and key laws, like the Ten Commandments. These carry the heart of a passage and get story cards and choice cards like anything else.

**The rule:** Papa Dorin sums up the stretch in one story card. He says in a few words what it covers and doesn't go item by item. He can name a standout detail the text gives if it helps the stretch stick. The baker should know the stretch is there and what it's about, not memorize it. A long stretch never gets a choice card.

### Story card examples: what Papa Dorin says

**Example 1: a stretch of rules for everyday life** *(Exodus 21–23)*

- ✅ **Good story card:** Papa Dorin says, "Then God gives Moses laws for everyday life: how to treat servants, what to do when someone gets hurt, and how to care for foreigners, widows and orphans."
- ❌ **Bad story cards:** Papa Dorin gives one card to the law about servants, another to the law about an ox that gores someone, another to the law about stolen sheep, and keeps going through all three chapters.

**Example 2: a genealogy** *(Genesis 5)*

- ✅ **Good story card:** Papa Dorin says, "From Adam down to Noah's father, each man lives hundreds of years, and then he dies. But not Enoch. Enoch walked with God, and then he was no more, because God had taken him away. The line comes down to Noah, who has three sons: Shem, Ham and Japheth."
- ❌ **Bad story cards:** Papa Dorin says, "Seth lived 912 years." Next card: "Enosh lived 905 years." Next card: "Kenan lived 910 years." And so on, one card per name.

In each good story card, the baker comes away knowing what the stretch was about, plus a detail worth remembering.

## How to word a choice card

Ask from inside the passage, the way a storyteller pauses to let the listener lean in. The question comes out of the moment Papa Dorin just set up.

### Choice card examples: what Papa Dorin sets up and asks

Each row is a separate example, in the order the moments happen in Jonah 1.

| ❌ Bad choice card | ✅ Good story card, then choice card |
|---|---|
| "What did Jonah do next?" | Story card: "God says go to Nineveh. Instead, Jonah runs to get away from the Lord. He goes down to Joppa and finds a ship." Choice card: "Where's it headed?" *(Tarshish, Jonah 1:3)* |
| "What happens next?" | Story card: "Jonah's on board, sailing away from the Lord. Then the Lord sends something after him." Choice card: "What is it?" *(a great wind, Jonah 1:4)* |
| "Which of these is true?" | Story card: "The storm is so bad the sailors are sure someone brought it on them." Choice card: "How do they find out who?" *(they cast lots, Jonah 1:7)* |
| "According to verse 17, what swallowed Jonah?" | Story card: "Over the side Jonah goes, into the sea. But the Lord isn't finished with him." Choice card: "What does He send?" *(a great fish, Jonah 1:17)* |

The bad ones sound like a test question. The good ones sound like Papa Dorin pausing mid-story.

## Poetry, letters and prophecy

A story has events the baker can reason about. A psalm, a letter or a prophet's message has wording the baker can only guess. So in these passages:

- **Ask about the movement and the meaning:** what changes, what the picture is, why the speaker says what he says.
- **Never ask the baker to guess the next words of a line.**
- **Fewer choice cards.** Tell more of the passage on story cards and save choice cards for the turns that matter.

### Example: Psalm 23

| ❌ Bad choice card | ✅ Good story card, then choice card |
|---|---|
| "He guides me in the paths of…" *(guessing the words: righteousness, peace or ancient?)* | Story card: "David's been resting in green pastures, beside quiet waters. Then the road turns: the valley of the shadow of death." Choice card: "Even there, David says he'll fear no evil. Why?" *(because the LORD is with him, 23:4)* |
| "What does He anoint David's head with?" *(guessing a detail: oil, myrrh or perfume?)* | Story card: "Halfway through, the picture changes. David isn't a sheep in a field anymore. He's a guest, and the LORD is the host." Choice card: "What does the LORD set up for him, right in front of his enemies?" *(a table, 23:5)* |

In the good ones, the baker can reason it out from what Papa Dorin just told them: a shepherd who stays with his sheep, a host who welcomes a guest.

## An informed guess, never luck

Prep must never become a guessing game.

- Before each choice card, Papa Dorin's story card gives enough context that the baker can reason their way to the answer.
- **The check:** could a thoughtful baker who has only seen the earlier cards make a reasoned pick? If the only way to get it right is luck, give the story card more context or ask a different question.
- **A few discovery questions are fine.** Some moments can't be reasoned out, like where Jonah's ship is headed. A few of those are okay, as long as most choice cards are informed guesses.

## Scripture rules (no inventing)

- Every story card cites the verses it tells.
- Every choice card and its right answer cite their verses. After a pick, the baker sees the reference, whether they were right or wrong.
- Papa Dorin never adds an event, detail, motive, feeling or line of dialogue that the passage doesn't state.
- If a detail isn't in the BSB text, it doesn't appear as fact.

## Answer rules

- Each choice card has **3 answers** and **one try**. A wrong pick shows the right answer with its verse, then play moves on.
- All three answers are the same kind of thing, about the same length and the same tone. None are silly.
- For who, what and where questions, wrong answers are real details from somewhere in the Bible, like Joppa when the right answer is Tarshish.
- For why and meaning questions, wrong answers only need to be believable.
- Each wrong answer must be false for that moment.
- Don't let the right answer stand out. All three answers sound alike. If one sounds like Bible wording and the others sound modern, rewrite them so they match.
- The code shuffles where the right answer sits. The agent doesn't need to.

## Cookies

- Every right answer adds **1 sugar cookie** to the box.
- A wrong answer adds nothing.
